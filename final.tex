\begin{abstract}
Independent-color resampling and RSW source shields imply an initial
ordered trace-germ zero-one law, with quantitative decorrelation between
small prefixes and the full exploration curve. This conclusion does not
assume affine-SLE marginals or a limiting domain Markov theorem.
Exact coalescence also gives total-variation prefix--suffix decoupling
and exponential mixing of annular source-crossing statistics, with a
centered strong law that does not require dilation invariance.
A universal tensor decoder at analytic regular endpoints, exact suffix
coalescence, and source splicing then show that a coherent family of
affine-SLE$_6$ mixtures must use one deterministic common affine metric.
The affine-mixture representation itself remains an unproved hypothesis.
Separately, a conditional Loewner-driving classification permits arbitrary
continuous finite-variation drift with a uniform first-order initial
tangent in probability; tightness in law along mirrored slit tests can
replace deterministic tangents. Locality and compatible strong DMP then
force Brownian driving of variance six. A wrong-affine-coordinate
obstruction holds under the weakened deterministic tangent assumptions.
A separate signed conditional-bias criterion supplies semimartingality
and the required initial tangents without postulating them; its discrete
version passes to continuous driver limits without filtration convergence.
The requisite bias cancellation and compatible limiting DMP remain unproved
for general percolation. These results leave the corrected three-axiom classification and critical
percolation universality open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Current locality and scope}
Two colored marked domains that agree near their common source must have
identical initial exploration laws until exit from that neighborhood,
even if their remote targets differ. This is the user's corrected locality
axiom at commit \texttt{0e47018}. We fix one color orientation throughout.
The terminal-germ construction for the earlier fixed-target axiom is
excluded; its original proofs are retained in

\section{A near-source drift-boundedness criterion}
\label{sec:strong-local-loewner}

\paragraph{Status.}
This is a new conditional rigidity result.  It does not assume conformal
invariance, Brownian driving, or local-martingale driving.  Instead it shows
that a locally bounded \emph{initial driving drift}, together with an
absolutely continuous drift part and locality, already forces zero drift and
variance six.  No regularity of quadratic variation is assumed.  Strong DMP then identifies the whole driving law.  The
boundedness and characteristic hypotheses are not derived from RSW or from
the three project axioms.  A finite-dimensional bulk-jet closure is given as
one explicit sufficient hypothesis.

Only a particular deletion consequence of the corrected source-neighborhood
locality is needed.  Arbitrary target changes and the full force of corrected
locality are not used in this theorem.  Thus the result is compatible with,
but does not exhaust, the corrected research objective.

\subsection{Chart-dependent initial characteristics and their covariance}

Let $f:\mathbb H\to D$ be an inverse chordal conformal chart, with source
$f(0)=p$ and target $f(\infty)=q$, both interpreted as prime ends.
For the physical ensemble in $D$, let $W^f$ be its driving process in this
chart, with half-plane capacity normalization
\[
 \partial_t g_t(z)=\frac{2}{g_t(z)-W^f_t},\qquad W^f_0=0.
\]
Assume that it is a continuous semimartingale in its natural trace filtration,
with decomposition
\[
 W^f_t=M^f_t+\int_0^t b^f_s\,ds,\qquad
 C^f_t=\langle M^f\rangle_t.
 \tag{DR1}
\]
The drift-density paths $b^f$ are assumed finite and right-continuous,
after local stopping if necessary.  No absolute continuity or regularity
of $C^f$ is assumed.  At the initial time suppose
\[
 b^f_0=B(f)\in\mathbb R
 \tag{DR2}
\]
is deterministic.  It may depend on the entire physical marked domain and
its conformal chart.  In particular no Markov property of the single real
driver is assumed.  A finite deterministic initial variance rate $V(f)$
will be \emph{derived} below from locality and (DR1)--(DR2).

Let $A\subset\overline{\mathbb H}$ be a half-plane hull disjoint from $0$,
let $h:\mathbb H\setminus A\to\mathbb H$ be hydrodynamically normalized,
and set
\[
 \bar h(z)=h(z)-h(0),\qquad f_A=f\circ\bar h^{-1}.
 \tag{DR3}
\]
The source is still the same physical prime end, since $\bar h(0)=0$;
the target is also unchanged.  Put $d=h'(0)>0$, $e=h''(0)$.
Source locality, together with the usual deletion-chart calculation, will
give a finite deterministic initial variance rate $V(f)$ and the covariance
\[
 \boxed{\quad V(f_A)=V(f),\qquad
 B(f_A)=\frac{B(f)}{d}
       +\left(\frac{V(f)}2-3\right)\frac{e}{d^2}.\quad}
 \tag{DR4}
\]
These are identities for \emph{initial} characteristics and do not identify
the two complete driving laws.

Here are the details without assuming a quadratic-variation density.
If $h_t$ is the evolving deletion map, then $du=h_t'(W_t)^2dt$, and
It\^o's formula gives
\[
 d\widehat W_{u(t)}
  =h_t'(W_t)\,dM_t+h_t'(W_t)b_t\,dt
       +\frac12 h_t''(W_t)\,dC_t-3h_t''(W_t)\,dt.
 \tag{DR5}
\]
The $-3$ comes from
\[
 \partial_t h_t(z)
 =\frac{2h_t'(W_t)^2}{h_t(z)-h_t(W_t)}
       -\frac{2h_t'(z)}{z-W_t},\qquad
 (\partial_t h_t)(W_t)=-3h_t''(W_t).
\]
The comparison of filtrations is justified by geometric invertibility:
from a deleted-domain initial trace one reconstructs its original trace and
capacity, so the inverse capacity clock is adapted and its inverse is a
stopping-time clock.  Locality therefore compares the same trace filtration,
not an unrelated enlargement.  Adaptedness of an arbitrary increasing clock
would not suffice for this step.

\paragraph{Local bracket-regularization lemma (proved).}
Suppose $h''(0)\ne0$.  Then on a positive initial interval, $C_t$ is absolutely
continuous and has a finite right-continuous density.  In particular its
initial right derivative $V(f)$ exists and is deterministic.

\begin{proof}
The deleted-domain driver also has an absolutely continuous drift,
$\int\widehat b_u\,du$, by (DR1).  Uniqueness of the continuous
semimartingale decomposition applied to (DR5), after the justified capacity
time change, gives the signed-measure identity
\[
 h_t''(W_t)\bigl(dC_t-6\,dt\bigr)
   =2\bigl[h_t'(W_t)^2\widehat b_{u(t)}
                      -h_t'(W_t)b_t\bigr]dt.
 \tag{DR5a}
\]
The second derivative starts nonzero and remains so on a positive initial
interval.  On that interval the bracket is absolutely continuous, with
\[
 v_t=6+2\frac{h_t'(W_t)^2\widehat b_{u(t)}
                         -h_t'(W_t)b_t}{h_t''(W_t)}.
 \tag{DR5b}
\]
The derivative paths of $h_t$ along the continuous driving point are
continuous, while the two drift-density paths are right-continuous and
finite.  Thus $v$ is right-continuous and finite there.  In particular
\[
 V(f)=v_0
   =6+2\frac{d^2 B(f_A)-d B(f)}e
 \tag{DR5c}
\]
is deterministic.  This is an intrinsic initial rate of $C$, so it does
not depend on which nonzero-$e$ deletion was used to derive it.
Applying the same time change to bracket gives
$V(f_A)=V(f)$.  Rearranging (DR5c) gives the drift identity (DR4).
\end{proof}

There is always an available nonzero-$e$ test deletion, for example one
vertical slit at any fixed real point different from $0$.  Right-continuity
makes the initial drift and variance rates intrinsic, rather than arbitrary
versions of densities at a single time.  The bracket density has only been
proved locally at the start; its global regularity is not being presumed.

\paragraph{Source-neighborhood convention.}
In half-plane coordinates these slit deletions preserve a full neighborhood
of the source end.  Under $f$ this is a prime-end neighborhood.  Thus (DR4)
is justified on arbitrary rough domains if the corrected locality assumption
is formulated for such neighborhoods, or if these particular deletions are
explicitly admissible.  For regular analytic source boundaries it is ordinary
Euclidean neighborhood locality.  If locality is instead stated only using
Euclidean balls at non-singleton source impressions, the all-rough-domain
extension must remain an explicit additional hypothesis.

\subsection{Two vanishing slits force both characteristics}

For $\epsilon>0$ take the reflected vertical slits
\[
 A_\epsilon^\pm
  =\{\,\pm\epsilon^2+iy:0\leq y\leq2\epsilon\,\}.
 \tag{DR6}
\]
Their hydrodynamic maps are
\[
 h_\epsilon^\pm(z)
 =\pm\epsilon^2+
   \sqrt{(z\mp\epsilon^2)^2+4\epsilon^2},
 \tag{DR7}
\]
with the square-root branch asymptotic to $z\mp\epsilon^2$ at infinity.
Let $f_\epsilon^\pm=f\circ
(h_\epsilon^\pm-h_\epsilon^\pm(0))^{-1}$.
Both sequences are conformal charts of bounded simply connected domains and
\[
 f_\epsilon^\pm\longrightarrow f
 \quad\text{locally uniformly in }\mathbb H.
 \tag{DR8}
\]
Indeed the slits have diameter $2\epsilon$ and approach $0$; directly from
(DR7), their maps and inverse maps converge to the identity on compact
subsets of $\mathbb H$, while $h_\epsilon^\pm(0)\to0$.
Crucially their boundary derivatives do \emph{not} converge to the identity
jet:
\[
 d_\epsilon=(h_\epsilon^\pm)'(0)
     =\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{\epsilon^2+4}}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 e_\epsilon^\pm=(h_\epsilon^\pm)''(0)
     =\mp\frac{4}{\epsilon(\epsilon^2+4)^{3/2}}.
 \tag{DR9}
\]
In particular
\[
 \frac{e_\epsilon^\pm}{d_\epsilon^2}
 =\mp\frac{4}{\epsilon^3\sqrt{\epsilon^2+4}}.
 \tag{DR10}
\]
Thus compact convergence of inverse charts permits very large second-jet
changes precisely at the source boundary.

\paragraph{Initial rigidity theorem (proved).}
Fix $f$ and assume (DR1)--(DR4) for $f$ and the charts $f_\epsilon^\pm$.
If
\[
 \sup_{0<\epsilon<\epsilon_0}
       \max\{|B(f_\epsilon^+)|,|B(f_\epsilon^-)|\}<\infty
 \tag{DR11}
\]
for some $\epsilon_0>0$, then
\[
 B(f)=0,\qquad V(f)=6.
 \tag{DR12}
\]
No continuity of $B$ is required.

\begin{proof}
Write $b=B(f)$, $v=V(f)$ and $b_\epsilon^\pm=B(f_\epsilon^\pm)$.
Equation (DR4) gives
\[
 b_\epsilon^\pm
 =\frac b{d_\epsilon}
    +\left(\frac v2-3\right)
                       \frac{e_\epsilon^\pm}{d_\epsilon^2}.
\]
Since $e_\epsilon^-=-e_\epsilon^+$,
\[
 b_\epsilon^++b_\epsilon^-=\frac{2b}{d_\epsilon},\qquad
 b_\epsilon^+-b_\epsilon^-=
       (v-6)\frac{e_\epsilon^+}{d_\epsilon^2}.
 \tag{DR13}
\]
The first right side is bounded only if $b=0$, since
$d_\epsilon\to0$.  The second is bounded only if $v=6$, since its multiplier
has magnitude asymptotic to $2\epsilon^{-3}$.  This proves (DR12).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exact instability and weaker rate conditions.}
For real numbers $U,C$ one has
$\max\{|U+C|,|U-C|\}=|U|+|C|$.  Hence the two drift covariances give the
exact identity
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \max\{|b_\epsilon^+|,|b_\epsilon^-|\}
 =\frac{|b|\sqrt{\epsilon^2+4}}{\epsilon}
  +\frac{4|v/2-3|}{\epsilon^3\sqrt{\epsilon^2+4}}.
 \quad}
 \tag{DR14}
\]
There is no possible cancellation between the two reflected tests.
In particular, if the displayed maximum is $o(\epsilon^{-3})$, then $v=6$.
If it is $o(\epsilon^{-1})$, then $b=0$ and $v=6$.
Boundedness is more than sufficient.  Conversely, if $v\ne6$, its leading
blow-up is $|v-6|\epsilon^{-3}$; if $v=6$ and $b\ne0$, its leading blow-up
is $2|b|\epsilon^{-1}$.  This is a concrete, quantitative obstruction to any
alternative semimartingale driving law with tame initial drift under source
perturbations.

\subsection{Local boundedness in marked-chart topology suffices}

Equip inverse marked charts with locally uniform convergence on
$\mathbb H$, retaining the fixed preimages $0,\infty$ of source and target.
If $B$ is locally bounded at every $f$ in this topology, then (DR8) implies
(DR11).  In particular continuity of $B$ is sufficient, but unnecessarily
strong.  This is a hypothesis about the driving characteristic itself;
weak convergence of curve laws alone does not imply it.

\paragraph{Conditional classification theorem (proved).}
Suppose a Borel family of continuous Loewner-generating exploration laws on
all bounded simply connected marked prime-end domains has strong DMP and
source-neighborhood locality (including the test deletions (DR6) in each
chart).  Suppose its drivers satisfy (DR1)--(DR2), with finite right-continuous
drift-density paths and the usual restart compatibility of characteristic
kernels, with no initial assumption on quadratic variation.  If the initial drift map $B$ is locally bounded in marked inverse-
chart topology, then every driving process is
\[
 W^f_t=\sqrt6\,B_t,
\]
and the family is chordal SLE$_6$ in these conformal coordinates.

\begin{proof}
The initial rigidity theorem gives $B(f)=0$ and the derived $V(f)=6$ for
every marked inverse chart.  At any deterministic rational time $r$,
condition on the initial exploration and use strong DMP in its remaining
marked domain and current conformal chart.  The centered future driver is
exactly the restarted driver; its initial drift is $b^f_r$, by
right-continuity.  The restarted law has initial drift zero, so
$b^f_r=0$ almost surely.  Countably many rational times and right-continuity
give $b^f_t=0$ throughout each localized lifetime.  Therefore all domain
chart drivers are continuous local martingales.

For completeness the no-density local-martingale locality argument then
identifies their brackets.  With both drifts zero, (DR5a) becomes
$h_t''(W_t)(dC_t-6\,dt)=0$.  A nonzero-$e$ vertical deletion yields
$C_t=6t$ on a positive initial interval.  Let
\[
 T=\inf\{t\geq0:C_t-6t\ne0\}.
\]
This is an entrance stopping time of a continuous adapted process into an
open set, in the usual right-continuous filtration.  The initial zero
interval gives $T>0$.  If $T<\infty$, continuity gives $C_T=6T$ and the
first-deviation definition gives nonzero values arbitrarily soon after $T$.
Strong DMP restarted at $T$ gives another positive zero interval, a
contradiction.  Thus $C_t=6t$ throughout.  L\'evy's characterization gives
Brownian driving, and Loewner generation identifies SLE$_6$.
\end{proof}

The Borel-kernel condition means that the family admits the standard strong
conditional-kernel interpretation on random slit domains, and that the
characteristics can be chosen consistently on that family.  It is not a
claim that deterministic-domain convergence of discrete explorations proves
this property.  Translation invariance is not needed in this conditional
theorem.  If its hypotheses hold only after one fixed physical affine
normalization, the conclusion is the corresponding fixed-affine SLE$_6$
family; an affine change of physical coordinates is not presumed to preserve
semimartingale driving in an arbitrary preselected complex structure.

\subsection{A finite-dimensional closure that is completely classified}

For example, fix finitely many interior points $z_j\in\mathbb H$ and
finitely many nonnegative derivative orders $m_j$.  Let
\[
 J(f)=\bigl(f^{(k)}(z_j):1\leq j\leq N,
                              0\leq k\leq m_j\bigr).
\]
Suppose the initial drift has a representation
\[
 B(f)=\mathcal B(J(f)),
 \tag{DR15}
\]
where $\mathcal B$ is locally bounded at every realized jet vector.
The bracket has no assumed density or finite-dimensional representation;
its initial rate is supplied by the local regularization lemma.
Locally uniform convergence of conformal maps implies convergence of all
these interior jets by Cauchy's formula, so (DR15) implies local boundedness
of $B$ in marked-chart topology.  The conditional classification theorem
therefore applies: within (DR1)--(DR2), every source-local strong-DMP family
with such a locally bounded finite-dimensional \emph{bulk-jet drift closure}
is SLE$_6$.

This statement does not cover a closure depending on unbounded source
boundary derivatives such as $f'(0)$ or a boundary pre-Schwarzian.  Those
quantities can change without control under (DR8), and can carry exactly the
singular terms in (DR13).  Nor does it show that true percolation limits have
semimartingale driving in a fixed physical complex structure.  The new
rigorous reduction is that any non-SLE candidate with regular characteristic
rates must violate the displayed near-source drift bound, in the explicit
$\epsilon^{-1}$ or $\epsilon^{-3}$ manner above.


\subsection{The criterion also detects a wrong affine coordinate system}

\paragraph{Corollary (proved).}
Let $A$ be an orientation-preserving real-linear map which is not a Euclidean
similarity, and let the physical ensemble be the fixed-affine pullback
\[
 P_{D,p,q}=(A^{-1})_*
       \operatorname{Law}(\mathrm{SLE}_6(AD;Ap,Aq)).
\]
Although this family satisfies corrected source-neighborhood locality and
strong DMP, in the original Euclidean complex structure it cannot satisfy
both the regular semimartingale characteristic assumptions (DR1)--(DR2) in
all marked domains and the locally bounded initial-drift condition above.

\begin{proof}
If both properties held, the conditional classification theorem would make
this ensemble ordinary SLE$_6$ in the original complex structure.  The
single-domain affine identifiability theorem would then make $A$ a
similarity, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

Thus the drift criterion selects a conformal structure.  It is not a
coordinate-free consequence of coarse crossing bounds: bounded affine
changes preserve such bounds, whereas a wrong affine structure necessarily
violates at least one of the criterion's additional analytic hypotheses.
The corollary does not decide whether the failure is semimartingality,
right-continuity of the drift density, deterministic initial drift,
or boundedness of the initial drift; distinguishing these failures is a
separate question.

\subsection{What corrected locality says at a regular analytic source germ}

Here is a separate necessary local structure statement that uses the full
corrected locality, including changes of the distant target.  It concerns
initial characteristics only, not existence of a process from arbitrary
coefficient choices.

Suppose the physical boundary is analytic near $p$ and the source colors
are fixed there.  Let $G$ denote this embedded colored source germ.  For a
chart $f$ extending conformally across $0$, define
\[
 a(f)=|f'(0)|>0,\qquad
 P(f)=\operatorname{Re}\frac{f''(0)}{f'(0)}.
 \tag{DR16}
\]
If another physical marked domain has the same source germ but any distant
target, let $k$ be its inverse chordal chart with $k(0)=p$.  The local map
$h=k^{-1}\circ f$ is analytic across $0$, takes the real boundary interval
to a real interval, and has $h(0)=0$, $d=h'(0)>0$, $e=h''(0)\in\mathbb R$.
The two initial physical traces agree by corrected locality.  The local
Loewner coordinate comparison is exactly (DR4), with the same derivation
from pole cancellation and It\^o's formula; a globally defined deletion map
is unnecessary.  Consequently $V(f)=V(k)$ depends only on $G$.

\paragraph{Proposition (proved).}
Under the semimartingale assumptions above, corrected locality at analytic
source germs is equivalent at the level of initial characteristic covariance
to the existence of two source-germ quantities $V(G)$ and $\beta(G)$ such that
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 V(f)=V(G),\qquad
 B(f)=a(f)\,\beta(G)
                 +\left(3-\frac{V(G)}2\right)P(f).
 \quad}
 \tag{DR17}
\]
Here ``equivalent at the level of covariance'' means precisely that every
covariant initial-characteristic assignment has this form, and every
assignment of this form obeys the local chart covariance identity.  It does
not mean that arbitrary $V,\beta$ yield a domain Markov exploration family.

\begin{proof}
Since $k=f\circ h^{-1}$, the chain rule gives
\[
 a(k)=\frac{a(f)}d,\qquad
 P(k)=\frac{P(f)}d-\frac e{d^2}.
\]
Write $c=V(G)/2-3$.  Combining these transformations with
$B(k)=B(f)/d+c e/d^2$ yields
\[
 \frac{B(k)+cP(k)}{a(k)}
       =\frac{B(f)+cP(f)}{a(f)}.
\]
This common value is the source-germ invariant $\beta(G)$, proving (DR17).
Conversely direct substitution gives the required covariance.
\end{proof}

Physical translation invariance makes $V(G)$ and $\beta(G)$ invariant under
translations of the colored germ.  If dilation covariance is separately
assumed, then $V(\lambda G)=V(G)$ and
$\beta(\lambda G)=\lambda^{-1}\beta(G)$.  For a straight source-boundary germ,
which is unchanged up to translation by dilation, this would force
$\beta(G)=0$; the dimensionless variance can still depend on the boundary
orientation before an affine metric has been identified.

The boundary pre-Schwarzian term in (DR17) explains the quantitative anomaly
in the vanishing-slit test.  Even when the physical source germ is unchanged,
$P(f_\epsilon)$ may grow as $\epsilon^{-3}$ while $f_\epsilon\to f$ on every
interior compact set.  Thus source locality by itself gives a connection-type
transformation law, rather than vanishing drift.  The locally bounded bulk
closure theorem removes exactly this uncontrolled boundary dependence.
The missing global step is to understand how DMP constrains these local
assignments at rough, dynamically created source germs, where the analytic
boundary jet in (DR16) no longer exists.

\section{Rigidity from an averaged initial finite-variation characteristic}
\label{sec:fv-tangent-rigidity}

\paragraph{Scope.}
This conditional theorem weakens the preceding driving-regularity premise.
The canonical finite-variation characteristic need not be absolutely
continuous and need not possess a right-continuous density. We assume only
a deterministic first-order tangent in probability, uniformly on shrinking
initial intervals. No such property is derived from RSW or the three axioms.

Let the driver in inverse chart $f$ have canonical decomposition
\[
 W^f=M^f+A^f,\qquad W^f_0=M^f_0=A^f_0=0,
 \tag{FV1}
\]
where $M^f$ is a continuous local martingale and $A^f$ is continuous,
adapted, and locally of finite variation. Write $C^f=\langle M^f\rangle$.
Assume for every deterministic state and inherited chordal chart that a
finite deterministic number $B(f)$ satisfies
\[
 \frac{1}{t}\sup_{0\le s\le t}|A^f_s-B(f)s|
       \xrightarrow[t\downarrow0]{\mathbb P}0.
 \tag{FV2}
\]
An almost-sure derivative $A^f_t/t\to B(f)$ implies (FV2), but (FV2)
is weaker. No bound on $\operatorname{TV}(A^f;[0,t])/t$ is assumed.

\begin{lemma}[A small finite-variation displacement in an analytic field]
\label{lem:fv-analytic-displacement}
Let $W=M+A$ as in (FV1), let $C=\langle M\rangle$, and suppose
$\sup_{s\le t}|A_s|=O_{\mathbb P}(t)$ and $A_t/t\to B$ in probability.
Let $H_t(x)$ be a random field analytic in $x$ on a fixed neighborhood of
zero for a positive initial time interval. Suppose it has absolutely
continuous finite variation in $t$, and, after localization, its first three
spatial derivatives and the first spatial derivative of $\partial_tH_t$
are bounded there. Assume $H_t'(x)\to d$ as $(t,x)\to(0,0)$.
Then
\[
 \frac1t\int_0^t H_s'(W_s)\,dA_s\longrightarrow dB
       \quad\hbox{in probability}.
 \tag{FV3}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Localize so $M,A,W$ stay in the spatial neighborhood. Apply the
finite-variation It\^o--Wentzell formula to
$F_t(x)=H_t(x+A_t)-H_t(x)$ at $x=M_t$. It gives
\begin{align*}
 \int_0^tH_s'(W_s)\,dA_s
 ={}&H_t(M_t+A_t)-H_t(M_t)\\
 &-\int_0^t[H_s'(M_s+A_s)-H_s'(M_s)]\,dM_s\\
 &-\frac12\int_0^t[H_s''(M_s+A_s)-H_s''(M_s)]\,dC_s\\
 &-\int_0^t[(\partial_sH_s)(M_s+A_s)
                  -(\partial_sH_s)(M_s)]\,ds.
\end{align*}
The first term divided by $t$ converges to $dB$ by the mean-value formula.
The second term is a continuous local martingale $N_t$ with bracket at
most $K^2\sup_{s\le t}|A_s|^2 C_t$. Thus $N_t/t\to0$ in probability:
stop when $|A_s|>Lt$ or $C_s>\eta$, apply the martingale maximal
inequality, then let $t\downarrow0$, $\eta\downarrow0$, and $L\uparrow\infty$.
Here $C_t\to0$ almost surely and $\sup|A_s|/t$ is tight. The third term
divided by $t$ is bounded by
$K\sup|A_s|C_t/t=o_{\mathbb P}(1)$; the fourth is bounded by
$K\sup|A_s|=o_{\mathbb P}(1)$. No total-variation estimate was used.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Initial covariance without a drift density]
\label{prop:fv-initial-covariance}
Suppose source locality allows one test deletion with hydrodynamic map $h$,
$d=h'(0)>0$, $e=h''(0)\ne0$, and both original and deleted drivers obey
(FV1)--(FV2), with their canonical characteristics in the same trace
filtration. Then
\[
 C^f_t/t\longrightarrow V(f)\in[0,\infty)
       \quad\hbox{in probability},
\]
where $V(f)$ is deterministic, and
\[
 V(f_A)=V(f),\qquad
 B(f_A)=\frac{B(f)}d+
            \left(\frac{V(f)}2-3\right)\frac e{d^2}.
 \tag{FV4}
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the same evolving deletion maps and stopping-time capacity clocks as
in (DR5), now without assuming a drift density. Put
$p_t=h_t'(W_t)$, $q_t=h_t''(W_t)$ and $u(t)=\int_0^t p_s^2ds$.
Uniqueness of the canonical semimartingale decomposition gives
\[
 \widehat A_{u(t)}
  =\int_0^t p_s\,dA_s+\frac12\int_0^tq_s\,dC_s
                                    -3\int_0^tq_s\,ds.
 \tag{FV5}
\]
The deletion field satisfies the analytic-field lemma after localization;
its Loewner field derivative and spatial derivatives are bounded near the
uncollided source. Therefore the first integral divided by $t$ tends to
$dB(f)$, without assuming $C_t/t$ is tight. Also $u(t)/t\to d^2$.
Condition (FV2) for the deleted state implies
$\widehat A_{u(t)}/t\to d^2B(f_A)$: on the event $u(t)\le Kt$, bound
the error by $t^{-1}\sup_{s\le Kt}|\widehat A_s-B(f_A)s|$, and choose
fixed $K>d^2$.

Since $q_t\to e\ne0$, (FV5) first implies $C_t/t$ is tight: with
probability tending to one $q_s$ has fixed sign and magnitude at least
$|e|/2$ on $[0,t]$, so $|\int q_s dC_s|\ge |e|C_t/2$.
Consequently
\[
 \frac1t\int_0^tq_s\,dC_s-e\frac{C_t}t
      \longrightarrow0
 \quad\hbox{in probability}.
\]
Taking (FV5) divided by $t$ now gives
\[
 V(f)=6+\frac{2\{d^2B(f_A)-dB(f)\}}e.
\]
This is intrinsic, nonnegative, and deterministic. The bracket relation
$\widehat C_{u(t)}=\int_0^t p_s^2dC_s$ gives $V(f_A)=V(f)$.
For this last step the tangents of $C,\widehat C$ may be evaluated at
asymptotically deterministic clocks by monotonic squeezing; convergence in
probability of an arbitrary signed process would not justify that step.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A finite-variation process with zero right tangent at every stopping time]
\label{lem:fv-zero-stopping-tangents}
Let $A_0=0$ be a continuous adapted locally finite-variation process. If
for every bounded stopping time $\tau$ before lifetime
\[
 \frac{A_{\tau+h}-A_\tau}{h}\longrightarrow0
       \quad\hbox{in probability as }h\downarrow0,
 \tag{FV6}
\]
then $A$ is indistinguishably zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Work on a compact localized interval. Pathwise put
$V_t=\operatorname{TV}(A;[0,t])$, and use its generalized inverse
$\theta(u)=\inf\{t:V_t\ge u\}$ for $0<u<V_T$. The usual arclength
parametrization of a continuous finite-variation path is
$G(u)=A_{\theta(u)}$: it is $1$-Lipschitz, satisfies
$A_t=G(V_t)$, and has $|G'(u)|=1$ for Lebesgue almost every $u$.
Indeed the variation of $G$ on every interval is its interval length.
The monotone function $\theta$ has a finite derivative
$\theta'(u)\in[0,\infty)$ for almost every $u$. Exclude also the
countably many levels corresponding to nontrivial flat intervals of $V$.

At a level where these properties hold, let $s=\theta(u)$ and
$v(h)=V_{s+h}-u$. Then $v(h)>0$ and $v(h)\to0$ as $h\downarrow0$.
The generalized-inverse bounds
$\theta((u+v(h))- )\le s+h\le\theta((u+v(h))+ )$
and differentiability of $\theta$ at $u$ give
$h/v(h)\to\theta'(u)$. Combining this with differentiability of $G$ gives
\[
 \lim_{h\downarrow0}\frac{A_{s+h}-A_s}{h}
 =\begin{cases}
 G'(u)/\theta'(u),&\theta'(u)>0,\\
 \operatorname{sgn}(G'(u))\,\infty,&\theta'(u)=0.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{FV7}
\]
Thus the extended right derivative is nonzero at almost every variation
level, including levels whose inverse belongs to a singular part of $dV$.

For deterministic $u>0$ use the stopping times
$\tau_u=\inf\{t:V_t\ge u\}$, truncated at a deterministic endpoint $T$.
Fubini and the preceding pathwise statement show that for Lebesgue
almost every deterministic $u$, on $\{\tau_u<T\}$ the quotient in
(FV6) has a nonzero or infinite pathwise limit almost surely.
But (FV6) gives convergence in probability to zero at this stopping time,
which forbids such an event of positive probability. Thus
$\mathbb P(\tau_u<T)=0$ for Lebesgue almost every $u$, and
$\mathbb E[V_T]=\int_0^\infty\mathbb P(\tau_u<T)du=0$ after localizing
$V_T$ to be integrable. Hence $V_T=0$ almost surely. Localization and a
countable exhaustion of the lifetime prove the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional classification from a finite-variation initial tangent]
\label{thm:fv-tangent-rigidity}
Suppose a Borel family on all relevant marked slit states has strong DMP
and source locality, including the same mirrored test deletions (DR6).
Suppose canonical decompositions (FV1), compatible with conditional restart
in the inherited chordal charts, obey (FV2). If $B(f)$ is locally bounded
in inverse-chart topology, or just satisfies the paired slit bound (DR11)
at every state, the family is chordal SLE$_6$ in these coordinates.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:fv-initial-covariance} gives exactly (DR4), hence
the reflected-slit sum and difference argument gives $B(f)=0$, $V(f)=6$
at every deterministic state.

At a stopping time $\tau$, the centered future driver is the driver of
the remaining physical state in the inherited conformal chart. Its
capacity parameter is exactly $s=t-\tau$ in the original half-plane
coordinate: recentering by $W_\tau$ introduces no additional scale clock.
Strong DMP and conditional-characteristic compatibility identify its
canonical finite-variation part with $A_{\tau+s}-A_\tau$ under the
regular conditional law. Applying (FV2) with $B=0$, and integrating these
conditional probabilities over the random state, gives (FV6).
Lemma~\ref{lem:fv-zero-stopping-tangents} therefore makes $A=0$.

The same conditional restart argument gives
$(C_{\tau+h}-C_\tau)/h\to6$ in probability at every stopping time.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:fv-zero-stopping-tangents} to the continuous
finite-variation process $C_t-6t$: it is identically zero.
Thus $W=M$ and $\langle M\rangle_t=6t$ globally.
L\'evy's characterization and continuous Loewner generation identify SLE$_6$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Strict weakening and remaining limitation.}
Even an absolutely continuous drift with density
$b_s=B+\sin(1/s)$, $s>0$, has no right limit at zero, whereas
$\int_0^t b_sds=Bt+O(t^2)$ and therefore satisfies (FV2).
The theorem also allows an a priori singular finite-variation drift;
its exclusion is a conclusion, not an assumption. A claim that an actual
exploration family has these initial characteristic tangents remains
unproved. The theorem still depends on choosing one correct physical
conformal structure and on tame behavior under the source slit tests.

\subsection{The wrong-coordinate obstruction also only needs finite-variation tangents}

\paragraph{Scaling lemma with probability tangents.}
The exact-scaling lemma (SM1)--(SM4) remains true if its drift assumption
is replaced by continuous finite variation and (FV2), and its bracket
assumption by $C_t/t\to v$ in probability for deterministic $v$.
Indeed (FV2) makes
$\sup_{s\le T}|c^{-1/2}A_{cs}|=O_{\mathbb P}(\sqrt c)\to0$.
Since $C$ is increasing, a finite deterministic mesh and monotonic
squeezing show
\[
 \sup_{s\le T}|C_{cs}/c-vs|\longrightarrow0
           \quad\hbox{in probability}.
\]
The continuous-martingale functional central limit argument in the earlier
scaling lemma then applies unchanged. Exact scaling makes the whole law
Brownian with variance $v$.

\paragraph{Local transfer of the tangent.}
In Proposition~\ref{prop:fv-initial-covariance}, the analytic-field
argument actually gives the uniform version
\[
 \frac1t\sup_{s\le t}
 \left|\int_0^s h_r'(W_r)\,dA_r-dB(f)s\right|
          \longrightarrow0
          \quad\hbox{in probability}.
\]
Use the martingale maximal inequality in its proof, and the same uniform
initial bound for $A$. Monotonic squeezing similarly makes
$C_{ts}/t\to V(f)s$ uniformly on compact $s$ intervals in probability.
Equation (FV5) therefore transfers (FV2) through a local analytic chart
change, once the initial bracket tangent has been obtained; it also
transfers its inverse, since the first chart derivative is positive.
No total-variation rate bound is introduced by this transfer.

\begin{corollary}[Wrong-affine obstruction under finite-variation tangents]
\label{cor:fv-wrong-affine}
Consider the canonical fixed-affine SLE$_6$ family with matrix $A$ on all
bounded marked domains. If every Euclidean chart driver admits (FV1)--(FV2),
then $A$ is a Euclidean similarity. No local boundedness of $B(f)$ is needed.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose the bounded flat-source domain and one test deletion used in the
bounded-domain obstruction theorem. Proposition~\ref{prop:fv-initial-covariance}
gives its deterministic bracket tangent in probability. Couple its initial
physical trace by known affine-SLE locality to the canonical half-plane
affine-SLE trace. The local analytic chart transfer just proved gives a
finite-variation decomposition and uniform deterministic drift tangent for
the half-plane driver, and the same deterministic bracket tangent.
Its exact Brownian scaling and the extended scaling lemma make its law
ordinary Brownian driving. The boundary-swallowing-race and affine
identification arguments in the half-plane obstruction theorem then give
variance six and force $A$ to be a similarity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A tightness-in-law variant without deterministic initial tangents}

\paragraph{Proposition (proved).}
There is a useful alternative formulation of the preceding theorem.
Allow (FV2) to have a finite random tangent $\beta^f$ rather than a
deterministic $B(f)$. Assume that for every fixed chart $f$ the family of
initial tangents in its two shrinking slit domains is uniformly tight:
\[
 \lim_{K\to\infty}\sup_{0<\epsilon<\epsilon_0}
 \max_{\sigma\in\{+,-\}}
 \mathbb P\{|\beta^{f_\epsilon^\sigma}|>K\}=0.
 \tag{FV8}
\]
Then the same classification follows, with all other family and restart
hypotheses unchanged.

The analytic-field lemma and initial covariance proof apply verbatim to
finite random tangents, producing an intrinsic finite random bracket
tangent $V^f$. For each fixed $\epsilon$, source locality and the
invertible local coordinate changes put the original and both deleted
initial traces on one probability space. Only their initial germs are
needed, so no simultaneous extension of this coupling past their exit times
is required. On that space the covariances give
\[
 \beta^{f_\epsilon^+}+\beta^{f_\epsilon^-}
       =2\beta^f/d_\epsilon,
 \qquad
 \beta^{f_\epsilon^+}-\beta^{f_\epsilon^-}
       =(V^f-6)e_\epsilon^+/d_\epsilon^2.
\]
Uniform tightness of the two marginals makes their sum and difference
uniformly tight under any coupling. Since $d_\epsilon\to0$ and
$|e_\epsilon^+|/d_\epsilon^2\to\infty$, these identities force
$\beta^f=0$ and $V^f=6$ almost surely. Formally, for $a>0$,
\[
 \mathbb P\{|\beta^f|>a\}
 \le \sum_{\sigma=\pm}
       \mathbb P\{|\beta^{f_\epsilon^\sigma}|>a/d_\epsilon\}
       \longrightarrow0,
\]
and the identical argument with threshold
$a|e_\epsilon^+|/(2d_\epsilon^2)$ gives $V^f=6$.
The stopping-time lemma then kills the finite-variation drift and identifies
the bracket as before. Local boundedness of a deterministic drift tangent
is one special case of (FV8); no deterministic initial-tangent premise is
otherwise necessary for this version.



\section{Rigidity from conditional first-moment bounds}
\label{sec:conditional-bias}

\paragraph{Scope.}
The next criterion replaces the a priori semimartingale and initial-tangent
premises by conditional first-moment bounds. It does not derive those bounds
from RSW. They must hold in one fixed physical conformal structure, with the stated control
along the specified shrinking slit tests. No quadratic-moment bound or
initial drift derivative is assumed.

\begin{lemma}[Conditional bias produces a bounded drift density]
\label{lem:cbias-doobmeyer}
Let $X$ be a bounded continuous adapted real process, with $X_0=0$, in a
usual filtration. Suppose a finite deterministic $K$ satisfies
\[
 \left|\mathbb E[X_t-X_s\mid\mathcal F_s]\right|
       \le K(t-s) \quad\hbox{almost surely},\qquad 0\le s\le t.
 \tag{BI1}
\]
Then $X=M+\int_0^\cdot b_rdr$, where $M$ is a continuous local martingale
and $b$ is predictable with $|b|\le K$ almost everywhere.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On a fixed finite time interval, $X_t+Kt$ and $-X_t+Kt$ are class-D
submartingales. Their Doob--Meyer decompositions have increasing predictable
parts $A^+$ and $A^-$. Adding the decompositions and using uniqueness gives
$A^++A^-=2Kt$. Thus $A^+$ and $A^-$ are absolutely continuous, with
densities in $[0,2K]$. The decomposition of $X$ has drift density
$b=dA^+/dt-K\in[-K,K]$. Since $X$ and its drift are continuous, its
martingale part is continuous. Uniqueness patches the decompositions over
finite time intervals.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Controlled rate bounds force the tangents to exist]
\label{prop:cbias-derived-tangents}
Use the mirrored shrinking slits $A_\epsilon^\pm$ in (DR6), with initial
derivatives $d_\epsilon$ and $e_\epsilon^\pm$ in (DR9). Assume the driver
in a state $f$, and the drivers in all these deleted states, have canonical
continuous-semimartingale decompositions with absolutely continuous drift.
In each state the drift density is bounded on some strictly positive
initial stopping interval. The interval may depend on the state and sample.
Let $K_\epsilon<\infty$ be a deterministic bound common to the two
deleted states. If $d_\epsilon K_\epsilon\to0$ along a sequence tending
to zero, then in the original state
\[
 \frac1t\sup_{s\le t}|A_s|\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \frac{C_t}{t}\longrightarrow6
 \quad\hbox{almost surely as }t\downarrow0.
 \tag{BI2}
\]
Here $A$ is canonical drift and $C$ is the martingale bracket. No right
continuity or initial tangent of the drift densities is presumed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By locality, compare the original and each deleted driver using the same
trace filtration and the stopping-time capacity clocks. With
$p_t^\sigma=(h_t^\sigma)'(W_t)$ and
$q_t^\sigma=(h_t^\sigma)''(W_t)$, uniqueness of canonical
characteristics gives
\[
 \frac12q_t^\sigma(dC_t-6dt)
   =\bigl[(p_t^\sigma)^2\widehat b^\sigma_{u_\sigma(t)}
                    -p_t^\sigma b_t\bigr]dt.
 \tag{BI3}
\]
One fixed nonzero-$q_0$ test makes $C$ absolutely continuous on a positive
initial interval; write $dC_t=v_tdt$ there. For each fixed $\epsilon$, both
mirrored identities hold almost everywhere on a positive initial interval,
as a two-by-two system in $b_t$ and $(v_t-6)/2$. Its coefficients converge
as $t\downarrow0$ to
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}d_\epsilon&e_\epsilon^+\\
                 d_\epsilon&-e_\epsilon^+\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose determinant is nonzero. The right sides are
$(p_t^\sigma)^2\widehat b^\sigma_{u_\sigma(t)}$, with each transformed
density bounded by $K_\epsilon$. Continuity of the coefficients and matrix
inversion therefore give the pathwise bounds
\[
 \mathop{\rm ess\,limsup}_{t\downarrow0}|b_t|\le d_\epsilon K_\epsilon,
 \qquad
 \mathop{\rm ess\,limsup}_{t\downarrow0}|v_t-6|
       \le \frac{2d_\epsilon^2K_\epsilon}{|e_\epsilon^+|}.
 \tag{BI4}
\]
These assertions concern the same original densities. Intersect the
probability-one sets for a countable sequence $\epsilon\downarrow0$.
By assumption $d_\epsilon K_\epsilon\to0$; also
$d_\epsilon/|e_\epsilon^+|\to0$, so both right sides tend to zero. Integrating the resulting essential right limits
proves (BI2). The positive comparison intervals may shrink with
$\epsilon$: the essential limsup is taken at fixed $\epsilon$ first.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[SLE from controlled conditional driving bias]
\label{thm:cbias-rigidity}
Let a Borel exploration family have continuous Loewner drivers on all
$t\ge0$, source locality including the test deletions, and strong DMP.
Require locality to identify the completed natural trace filtrations via
the invertible capacity clocks. Under conditional restart in an inherited
chart, require the canonical martingale, drift, and bracket to coincide
with those of the restarted kernel. These are the same explicit
filtration and characteristic compatibility premises as in the earlier
conditional driving theorems. Suppose that, in every
deterministic state $f$, there is an increasing sequence of stopping times
$\rho_{f,m}$ increasing almost surely to infinity, with $\rho_{f,1}>0$ almost surely,
such that each $X^{f,m}_t=W^f_{t\wedge\rho_{f,m}}$ is bounded and
satisfies (BI1), with a finite deterministic bound $K(f,m)$. Require
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\epsilon_n
          \max_{\sigma=\pm}K(f_{\epsilon_n}^\sigma,1)=0
 \tag{BI5}
\]
at every state, for some sequence $\epsilon_n\downarrow0$. In particular uniformly
bounded constants suffice; growth $o(\epsilon_n^{-1})$ is allowed.
Then the family is chordal SLE$_6$ in these coordinates.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:cbias-doobmeyer}, applied to the localizing sequence, gives
global continuous-semimartingale decompositions with locally bounded
absolutely continuous drift. Uniqueness patches the decompositions on
overlapping stopped intervals. Proposition~\ref{prop:cbias-derived-tangents}
then gives the zero drift tangent and bracket tangent six in every state.

Use the conditional-kernel compatibility of canonical decompositions in
the inherited natural trace filtration, as in the earlier conditional
characteristic theorems. At every
bounded stopping time $\tau$, conditional DMP and (BI2) give
\[
 \frac{A_{\tau+h}-A_\tau}{h}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \frac{C_{\tau+h}-C_\tau}{h}\longrightarrow6
 \quad\hbox{in probability}.
\]
The zero-stopping-tangent lemma
(Lemma~\ref{lem:fv-zero-stopping-tangents}) applied to $A$ and $C-6t$
gives $A=0$ and $C_t=6t$. L\'evy's characterization identifies the
driver as $\sqrt6 B$, hence the trace as SLE$_6$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Rates in the slit test.}
Since $d_\epsilon\sim\epsilon/2$ and
$|e_\epsilon^+|\sim1/(2\epsilon)$, the two bounds in (BI4)
are of orders $\epsilon K_\epsilon$ and
$\epsilon^3 K_\epsilon$. The weaker condition
$\epsilon^3K_\epsilon\to0$ alone forces the initial bracket tangent
to be six. The full classification above also needs the stronger drift
condition in (BI5).

\paragraph{What this reduction gains and what it leaves open.}
Conditional bias bounds concern first moments of observable driver
increments, rather than unknown canonical decompositions. They produce
semimartingality, bounded drift densities, and the missing initial tangents
without assuming quadratic variation estimates. They are nevertheless
substantial cancellation estimates: a bound on
$\mathbb E|W_{t+h}-W_t|$ of Brownian order $\sqrt h$, or tightness from
RSW, does not imply (BI1). The rate control in (BI5) is coordinate sensitive
and is not justified by convergence of curve laws in the shrinking slit
domains. Thus this is a conditional route toward the desired regularity,
not a proof of percolation universality.

\subsection{An observable discrete criterion that passes to curve limits}
\label{sec:discrete-bias-transfer}

There is no need to identify the discrete or limiting canonical
characteristics in order to test the preceding criterion. Signed
conditional first moments on a discrete exploration clock suffice.
The following elementary transfer also avoids convergence of filtrations.
It does not establish the separate strong-DMP premise of the rigidity
theorem.

\begin{proposition}[Discrete conditional bias passes to the natural limit filtration]
\label{prop:discrete-bias-transfer}
Fix $T>0$. For each $n$, let
$0=t^n_0<t^n_1<\cdots<t^n_{N_n}=T$ be a deterministic partition
with maximum spacing tending to zero. Let $X^n$ be its continuous
linear interpolation of grid values, all bounded by one deterministic
constant $L$. Suppose the grid values are adapted to filtrations
$(\mathcal G^n_j)_j$ and
\[
 \left|\mathbb E\left[X^n_{t^n_{j+1}}-X^n_{t^n_j}
                         \mid\mathcal G^n_j\right]\right|
 \le (K+\delta_n)(t^n_{j+1}-t^n_j),\qquad\delta_n\downarrow0.
 \tag{DB1}
\]
Suppose $X^n$ converges in distribution in $C([0,T])$ to $X$.
Then in the usual augmentation of the natural filtration of $X$,
\[
 \left|\mathbb E[X_t-X_s\mid\mathcal F_s]\right|
       \le K(t-s),\qquad 0\le s\le t\le T.
 \tag{DB2}
\]
Consequently $X$ has a continuous local-martingale part and an
absolutely continuous canonical drift with density bounded by $K$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Summing the conditional inequalities and using the tower property
shows that the grid processes
$X^n_{t^n_j}\pm(K+\delta_n)t^n_j$ are respectively a submartingale
and a supermartingale. In particular, for grid points $s_n\le t_n$
and every bounded nonnegative $\mathcal G^n_{j(s_n)}$-measurable $H_n$,
\[
 \left|\mathbb E[(X^n_{t_n}-X^n_{s_n})H_n]\right|
       \le (K+\delta_n)(t_n-s_n)\mathbb E H_n.
 \tag{DB3}
\]
Choose grid points $s_n\to s$ and $t_n\to t$, with $s_n\le t_n$,
and set $H_n=H(X^n_{\cdot\wedge s_n})$, where $H$ is any bounded
nonnegative continuous function on $C([0,T])$.
The stopped interpolated path is measurable at the grid time $s_n$.
Convergence in $C([0,T])$ and uniform continuity of each limiting
path give joint convergence of the evaluations and stopped paths.
The deterministic bound $L$ permits passage of their expectations to
the limit in (DB3).

Bounded continuous tests determine finite measures on the stopped-path
space. Thus the resulting two inequalities extend to all bounded
nonnegative tests measurable in the raw natural filtration at $s$.
This proves (DB2) there. Equivalently $X_t+Kt$ and $-X_t+Kt$ are
continuous class-D submartingales. These remain submartingales under
the usual augmentation: for $u\downarrow s$, apply the inequality
at $u<t$, then use reverse-martingale convergence and continuity of
$X$ to condition on $\bigcap_{u>s}\mathcal F_u^{\rm raw}$.
Completion changes only null sets. Lemma~\ref{lem:cbias-doobmeyer}
now supplies the asserted canonical decomposition.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exploration-time variant.}
An equivalent usable sufficient hypothesis allows a random discrete
clock. Let the successive capacity times $t^n_j$ and grid values
$X^n_j$ be adapted to the discrete revealed-color filtration, with
uniform deterministic bounds on the drivers and clock horizons. Assume
the times are nondecreasing, interpolation is well defined, and
$J_n(s)=\inf\{j:t^n_j\ge s\}<\infty$ almost surely for each $s<T$.
Suppose their largest spacing tends to zero in probability and
\[
 \left|\mathbb E[X^n_{j+1}-X^n_j\mid\mathcal G^n_j]\right|
 \le (K+\delta_n)
       \mathbb E[t^n_{j+1}-t^n_j\mid\mathcal G^n_j].
 \tag{DB4}
\]
After continuous interpolation, assume the same uniform-path convergence.
Then (DB2) again follows. Indeed $X^n_j\pm(K+\delta_n)t^n_j$
are bounded discrete sub/supermartingales. Use optional sampling at
$J_n(s)=\inf\{j:t^n_j\ge s\}$ and $J_n(t)$, with a test
of the path stopped at $t^n_{J_n(s)}$. The indices are stopping times
because $\{J_n(s)\le j\}=\{t^n_j\ge s\}$.
The clock overshoots tend to zero in probability; tightness in the
continuous-path topology makes the stopped paths and sampled values
converge to their deterministic-time counterparts. Boundedness then
passes the inequalities to the limit as above. At $T$, use continuity
from times below $T$.

\paragraph{Fixed-interval block estimates also suffice.}
The per-reveal condition (DB4) is sufficient, not necessary for this
transfer argument. It is enough that for each fixed $s<t$ and every
bounded nonnegative continuous stopped-path test $H$, the tested
inequalities (DB3), using approximations of $s,t$, hold up to an error
$o(1)\|H\|_\infty$. The same weak-convergence proof then gives
(DB2). Thus one may instead seek signed cancellation over mesoscopic
blocks, allowing microscopic biases to cancel before a fixed capacity
interval. No per-reveal estimate is asserted for lattice percolation.

Thus a possible lattice-level research target is the signed cancellation
estimate (DB4), after suitable bounded stopping, in an unknown but fixed
affine normalization. The constants must satisfy the shrinking-slit
control in Theorem~\ref{thm:cbias-rigidity}, and the limit must separately
have its stated strong-DMP and conditional-characteristic compatibility.
Neither a Brownian-size absolute increment estimate nor RSW alone is
being substituted for this signed conditional estimate.


\section{A wrong affine metric cannot have tame driving drift in every bounded domain}
\label{sec:scaling-semimartingale}

\paragraph{Status.}
The conclusion here is stronger than the earlier wrong-coordinate consequence
of drift boundedness.  For a fixed non-similarity affine deformation of SLE$_6$,
regular semimartingale driving with absolutely continuous right-continuous
drift cannot hold in every bounded domain in the wrong Euclidean complex
structure.  No local boundedness of the initial drift functional is needed.
The proof uses exact scale invariance only for the \emph{known fixed-affine
SLE$_6$ half-plane law}; it does not assume that arbitrary critical
subsequential limits are scale invariant.  All characteristic regularity
assumptions remain explicit.

\subsection{Exact Brownian scaling and a regular initial bracket}

\paragraph{Scaling lemma (proved).}
Let $W$ be a continuous real process on $[0,\infty)$ with $W_0=0$, whose law
has exact Brownian scaling:
\[
 (c^{-1/2}W_{ct})_{t\geq0}\ \stackrel{\rm law}{=}\ (W_t)_{t\geq0}
 \qquad(c>0).
 \tag{SM1}
\]
Suppose up to a strictly positive stopping time it is a continuous
semimartingale
\[
 W_t=M_t+\int_0^t b_s\,ds,
 \tag{SM2}
\]
where $M_0=0$ is a continuous local martingale and $b$ is finite and
right-continuous at $0$.  Suppose its bracket has a finite deterministic
initial right derivative
\[
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{\langle M\rangle_t}{t}=v\in[0,\infty)
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{SM3}
\]
Then the entire law of $W$ is that of $\sqrt v B$.  This includes the
identically zero process if $v=0$.

\begin{proof}
Fix $T<\infty$ and stop the local decomposition at a positive initial
localizing time.  As $c\downarrow0$, with probability tending to one the
whole interval $[0,cT]$ lies before that time.  Right-continuity of $b$ gives
\[
 \sup_{t\leq T}\left|c^{-1/2}\int_0^{ct}b_s\,ds\right|
 \leq\sqrt c\,T\sup_{s\leq cT}|b_s|\longrightarrow0
\]
almost surely on the localized event.  Writing $C_t=\langle M\rangle_t$,
(SM3) implies
\[
 \sup_{t\leq T}|C_{ct}/c-vt|\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{SM4}
\]
Indeed $|C_{ct}/c-vt|\leq t\sup_{0<s\leq cT}|C_s/s-v|$.
The continuous-martingale functional central limit theorem now gives
$c^{-1/2}M_{c\cdot}\Rightarrow\sqrt v B$ on $C[0,T]$.
One may see this directly from Dambis--Dubins--Schwarz: extend its Brownian
motion if necessary and write
\[
 c^{-1/2}M_{ct}=B^{(c)}_{C_{ct}/c},
 \qquad B^{(c)}_u=c^{-1/2}\widetilde B_{cu}.
\]
Each $B^{(c)}$ has standard Brownian law.  Uniform bracket convergence to the
deterministic clock and the Brownian modulus of continuity imply the stated
limit; independence between $B^{(c)}$ and the bracket is unnecessary.
Adding the negligible drift gives the same limit for $W$.
By exact scaling, however, each rescaled process already has the law of
$W$ on $[0,T]$.  That law is therefore Brownian with variance $v$.
Arbitrariness of $T$ proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

The regular initial bracket is not automatic for a general self-similar
continuous process.  In the affine-SLE application below it follows from
locality and absolutely continuous drift in two domain charts, using the
local bracket-regularization identity.

\subsection{The half-plane affine-SLE obstruction}

Let $A$ be an invertible orientation-preserving real-linear map and let
$\eta$ be chordal SLE$_6$ in $A\mathbb H$ from $0$ to $\infty$.  Put
$\gamma=A^{-1}\eta\subset\overline{\mathbb H}$ and let $W$ be its
half-plane-capacity driving function.  Its continuous Loewner generation and
infinite capacity lifetime are supplied by the affine-admissibility result
already proved in the research record.  Its law has exact Brownian scaling
(SM1): ordinary SLE is dilation covariant, $A$ commutes with positive scalar
dilations, and capacity scales by the square of physical length.

\paragraph{Proposition (proved).}
If this $W$ satisfies (SM2)--(SM3), then $A$ is a Euclidean similarity.

\begin{proof}
The scaling lemma makes $W$ ordinary Brownian driving of variance $v$.
Thus the trace is ordinary chordal SLE$_v$ in $\mathbb H$.
We identify $v$ without a Hausdorff-dimension theorem.

For $\kappa\leq4$ the nonzero real boundary points are never swallowed by
SLE$_\kappa$: their Loewner distances divided by $\sqrt\kappa$ are Bessel
processes of dimension $1+4/\kappa\geq2$ (the case $\kappa=0$ is immediate).
An affine image of SLE$_6$ swallows boundary points, so $v>4$.
For any $\kappa>4$, the boundary-race calculation gives
\[
 \mathbb P_\kappa(\tau_y<\tau_x)
 =F_\kappa\!\left(\frac{-x}{y-x}\right),\qquad
 F_\kappa(r)=
 \frac{\displaystyle\int_0^r[u(1-u)]^{-4/\kappa}\,du}
 {\displaystyle\int_0^1[u(1-u)]^{-4/\kappa}\,du},
 \quad x<0<y.
 \tag{SM5}
\]
This includes $\kappa\geq8$.  To check it, the ratio
$R=-X/(Y-X)$ has generator
\[
 \frac\kappa2\frac{d^2}{dr^2}
   +\frac{2(1-2r)}{r(1-r)}\frac d{dr}
\]
after $ds=dt/(Y-X)^2$.  Its scale density is
$[r(1-r)]^{-4/\kappa}$, integrable at both ends exactly when $\kappa>4$.
The first swallowing is finite almost surely by the Bessel description;
simultaneous swallowing is impossible since $Y-X$ is positive and
nondecreasing.  Bounded optional stopping gives (SM5).

Rotate $A\mathbb H$ conformally back to $\mathbb H$.  The resulting map
has matrix
\[
 T=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\0&\delta\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \alpha,\delta>0.
\]
It acts on the real boundary by $x\mapsto\alpha x$, so it preserves the
ratio $-x/(y-x)$ and the topological swallowing order.  The law of
$\gamma$ therefore has boundary-race function $F_6$, while its Brownian
driving gives $F_v$.  Equality for every $r\in(0,1)$ forces $v=6$:
compare the derivatives in (SM5), proportional respectively to
$[r(1-r)]^{-4/v}$ and $[r(1-r)]^{-2/3}$.

Finally $T^{-1}\mathrm{SLE}_6(\mathbb H;0,\infty)$ is ordinary SLE$_6$.
Half-plane affine identifiability now implies $T$ is a similarity.
For completeness, the strictly increasing SLE$_6$ left-passage function
\[
 L(u)=\frac{\int_{-\infty}^u(1+s^2)^{-2/3}\,ds}
 {\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(1+s^2)^{-2/3}\,ds}
\]
and preservation of topological side give
$L(u)=L((\alpha u+\beta)/\delta)$ for all real $u$.
Thus $\alpha=\delta$ and $\beta=0$.  Rotation does not affect whether $A$
is a similarity, proving the proposition.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Transfer from bounded-domain regularity to the half-plane germ}

The preceding proposition is already a complete unbounded-domain statement.
The following additional argument shows why requiring the tame drift
structure in \emph{every bounded domain} is enough to trigger it.
We spell out the local coordinate transfer, rather than silently assume
semimartingality is preserved by a general physical affine map.

\paragraph{Bounded-domain obstruction theorem (proved).}
Consider the canonical fixed-affine SLE$_6$ family
\[
 P_{D,p,q}=(A^{-1})_*\operatorname{Law}
       (\mathrm{SLE}_6(AD;Ap,Aq))
\]
on bounded simply connected marked domains.  Suppose in the original
Euclidean complex structure every domain-chart driver is a continuous
semimartingale whose finite-variation part is
$\int_0^t b_s\,ds$, with finite right-continuous drift density and
deterministic initial value $B(f)$, consistently under the usual conditional
kernel interpretation.  Then $A$ is a Euclidean similarity.
No boundedness or continuity of the map $f\mapsto B(f)$ is assumed.

\begin{proof}
Choose a bounded half-disk $D\subset\mathbb H$ with $0$ in the interior of
its real diameter, and a distinct target on that diameter away from $0$.
Choose the boundary colors so they agree near $0$ with the half-plane
problem $(\mathbb H,0,\infty)$.  Let $\phi:D\to\mathbb H$ be its chordal
chart sending these two marks to $0,\infty$.  It extends analytically
across $0$, with $\phi'(0)>0$.

The local bracket-regularization lemma applies to this bounded-domain
driver: delete one further vertical slit in its $\phi$ chart, away from its
marks, with nonzero $h''(0)$.  Both domains have tame absolutely continuous
drift by hypothesis.  The signed-measure identity
\[
 h_t''(W_t)(dC_t-6\,dt)
  =2\bigl[h_t'(W_t)^2\widehat b_{u(t)}
                        -h_t'(W_t)b_t\bigr]dt
\]
therefore gives a finite right-continuous initial bracket density in $D$,
with deterministic initial value $v_D$.

Corrected source-neighborhood locality of ordinary SLE$_6$, preserved by
the fixed map $A^{-1}$, couples this bounded-domain curve to the canonical
affine-SLE$_6$ half-plane curve so that their physical initial segments are
identical until leaving a sufficiently small half-neighborhood of $0$.
This uses ordinary SLE$_6$ locality including distant target changes; it is
not a new hypothesis about an unknown nonconformal family.
On that common initial trace, the coordinate change is the analytic map
$\phi$ at the source.

A local analytic coordinate change and its inverse preserve the tame
semimartingale structure just obtained.  More explicitly, if $t$ is physical
half-plane capacity and $u$ is the bounded-chart capacity, the evolving local
map satisfies
\[
 W_D(u(t))=h_t(W_H(t)),\qquad
 \dot u(t)=h_t'(W_H(t))^2.
\]
Initially $h_0=\phi$, so $h_0'(0)>0$ and all required local derivatives are
finite.  Before the trace leaves the common neighborhood, localize so the
first derivative stays bounded away from zero and the derivatives stay
bounded.  Both capacity clocks are adapted functions of the same initial
trace; their inverses are stopping-time clocks.  The inverse field
$h_t^{-1}$ has finite variation in time, as follows by differentiating the
Loewner composition.  Applying the finite-variation It\^o--Wentzell formula
to the semimartingale $W_D$, and changing the capacity clock, produces a
continuous-semimartingale decomposition of $W_H$ on a positive initial
interval.  Its drift is absolutely continuous and right-continuous, and its
initial bracket density is the same scalar $v_D$.  These conclusions use
only a \emph{conformal local chart transition}, not the nonconformal map $A$.

For an explicit initial check, writing $d=\phi'(0)$, $e=\phi''(0)$,
the transferred rates obey
\[
 V_H=v_D,\qquad
 B_H=dB_D-\left(\frac{v_D}{2}-3\right)\frac ed,
 \tag{SM6}
\]
so both are finite and deterministic.  Thus the canonical half-plane driver
satisfies (SM2)--(SM3).  Its exact scaling and the half-plane proposition
force $A$ to be a similarity.
\end{proof}

Only a bounded domain flat at its source and one of its source-preserving
slit deletions are needed for the bracket part of this contradiction.
The full all-bounded-domain assumption is a convenient sufficient
formulation.  The obstruction does not identify the precise failure for a
particular wrong affine metric: semimartingality, absolute continuity or
right-continuity of the drift, or deterministic initial drift may fail.
It does show that at least one must fail somewhere.  It supplies no argument
that an unknown exploration family admits a correct affine normalization
with tame drift.

\section{An affine metric is identifiable from one chordal law}
\label{sec:identifiability}

For a bounded Jordan domain $D$, distinct $p,q\in\partial D$, and an
orientation-preserving invertible real-linear map $A$, put
\begin{equation}
 \nuA{A}{D}{p}{q}
 = (A^{-1})_*\Law\bigl(\SLE(AD;Ap,Aq)\bigr).
 \label{eq:affine-law}
\end{equation}
Translations in an affine normalizer are immaterial. The tensor
$A^{\mathsf T}A$ is its Euclidean conformal metric, determined only up to a
positive scalar.

\subsection{A boundary-swallowing race}

\begin{lemma}[Strict boundary-race probability]
\label{lem:race}
Let $\eta$ be chordal SLE$_6$ in $\HH$ from $0$ to $\infty$. For
$x<0<y$, let $\tau_x,\tau_y$ denote their Loewner swallowing times. Then
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P(\tau_y<\tau_x)=F\left(\frac{-x}{y-x}\right),\qquad
 F(r)=\frac{\int_0^r[u(1-u)]^{-2/3}\,du}
 {\int_0^1[u(1-u)]^{-2/3}\,du}.
 \label{eq:race}
\end{equation}
In particular $F$ is strictly increasing on $(0,1)$. The event in
\eqref{eq:race} is invariant under orientation-preserving homeomorphisms of
marked Jordan domains, when formulated as the order of separation from
the terminal prime end by the filled exploration hull.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use $\partial_tg_t(z)=2/(g_t(z)-W_t)$ with $W_t=\sqrt6 B_t$. Until the
first swallowing time put
\[
 X_t=g_t(x)-W_t<0,\quad Y_t=g_t(y)-W_t>0,\quad
 T_t=Y_t-X_t,\quad R_t=-X_t/T_t\in(0,1).
\]
Then
\begin{align*}
 dX_t&=\frac{2}{X_t}\,dt-\sqrt6\,dB_t,&
 dY_t&=\frac{2}{Y_t}\,dt-\sqrt6\,dB_t,\\
 dT_t&=\frac{2T_t}{-X_tY_t}\,dt>0,&
 dR_t&=\frac{2(1-2R_t)}{R_t(1-R_t)T_t^2}\,dt
                 +\frac{\sqrt6}{T_t}\,dB_t.
\end{align*}
With $ds=dt/T_t^2$, the generator of $R$ is
\[
 \mathcal L=3\frac{d^2}{dr^2}
       +\frac{2(1-2r)}{r(1-r)}\frac d{dr}.
\]
The increasing solution of $\mathcal LF=0$, $F(0)=0$, $F(1)=1$ has
$F'(r)=C[r(1-r)]^{-2/3}$, giving \eqref{eq:race} by bounded optional
stopping. To justify the terminal values, $|X|/\sqrt6$ and $Y/\sqrt6$
are Bessel processes of dimension $1+4/6=5/3$ up to their first zero;
thus the first swallowing time is finite almost surely. Alternatively,
the accessible endpoints of the displayed diffusion give the same stopping
argument. There is no simultaneous swallowing, since $T_t\geq y-x>0$,
whereas both $X_t,Y_t\to0$ would force $T_t\to0$. At the first swallowing,
$R=1$ exactly when $y$ is swallowed first. ODE solutions and the continuous
driver stay finite up to that first time.

Swallowing means that the marked boundary end has been cut off from the
target by the explored filled hull (including the boundary swallowed at
first contact). This definition uses only the domain, the oriented path,
and its order of traversal. A homeomorphism carries filled target
components to filled target components. Capacity parametrization therefore
has no role in the comparison event.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Boundary rigidity and affine uniqueness}

\begin{lemma}[A single SLE law identifies its conformal boundary map]
\label{lem:boundary-rigidity}
Let $h:\overline D\to\overline{D'}$ be an orientation-preserving
homeomorphism between closed bounded Jordan domains, with
$p'=h(p)$, $q'=h(q)$. If $h_*\SLE(D;p,q)=\SLE(D';p',q')$, then
$h|_{\partial D}$ agrees with the boundary extension of a conformal
map $f:D\to D'$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose conformal maps $\phi:D\to\HH$ and $\phi':D'\to\HH$ sending
their marked endpoints to $0,\infty$. Their Jordan boundary extensions
give an increasing homeomorphism
\[
 H=\phi'\circ h\circ\phi^{-1}:\overline\RR\to\overline\RR
\]
fixing $0,\infty$. For every $x<0<y$, equality of the corresponding
boundary-race probabilities and strictness in Lemma~\ref{lem:race} give
\[
 \frac{-H(x)}{H(y)-H(x)}=\frac{-x}{y-x},\quad\text{hence}\quad
 \frac{H(x)}{H(y)}=\frac{x}{y}.
\]
Fixing one $x<0$ and one $y>0$ shows $H(s)=cs$ for all real $s$, with
$c>0$. Consequently $f=(\phi')^{-1}\circ(c\phi)$ has exactly the
required boundary values. Equality of interior maps is not asserted for
an arbitrary $h$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Single-domain affine identifiability]
\label{thm:affine-identifiability}
For even one bounded Jordan $D$ and one pair of distinct boundary points
$p,q$, the equality $\nuA{A}{D}{p}{q}=\nuA{B}{D}{p}{q}$ holds if and
only if $BA^{-1}$ is a Euclidean similarity. Equivalently,
\[
 B^{\mathsf T}B=c A^{\mathsf T}A\qquad(c>0).
\]
The statement also allows orientation-reversing normalizers by composing
one map with a reflection.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The equality of laws means $M=BA^{-1}$ carries the SLE law in $AD$ to
the SLE law in $BD$, at the corresponding endpoints. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary-rigidity}, a conformal map $f:AD\to BD$ has
boundary values equal to $M$. Both real coordinate functions of the
real-linear map $M$ are harmonic. The real and imaginary parts of $f$
are also harmonic, continuous on the closure, with the same boundary
values. The maximum principle on the bounded domain gives $f=M$
throughout $AD$. Thus $M$ is complex-linear and invertible, hence a
similarity. The converse is ordinary SLE similarity covariance.
Reflection covariance reduces the remaining orientation case to this one.
\end{proof}


\section{An affine tensor is readable from a regular endpoint germ}
\label{sec:regular-endpoint-decoder}

\paragraph{Status.}
The argument below extends the initial half-plane trace-germ decoder to
analytic regular source germs. By ordinary SLE$_6$ reversibility, it gives
a decoder from the ordered terminal trace germ at any analytic regular
target, even when the other endpoint and the rest of the domain are rough.
It does not decode the unlabelled accessible frontier at a random tip.
All almost-sure assertions are made separately under each fixed affine
tensor; no uncountable intersection of full-probability events is used.

Let $\mathcal X$ be the usual Polish space of oriented unparameterized
continuous finite curves, with the uniform Fr\'echet metric. Curves are
identified under increasing reparameterization, including deletion of
pauses. For a continuous path $Y$ starting at $0$ and exiting every disk,
write $\tau_a(Y)$ for its first hit of $\{|z|=a\}$ and
$\operatorname{Stop}_aY=Y[0,\tau_a(Y)]$.
The operations used here are Borel in standard continuous-curve coding;
first hits can equivalently be defined by rational-time approximation.
For a rough opposite prime end the full curve need not land continuously
there. Only finite prefixes near the decoded regular endpoint are coded
in $\mathcal X$; the unbounded half-plane curves exit every finite disk.
This avoids any assumption of a singleton impression at the other mark.

\begin{lemma}[Unit-radius stopping is almost surely continuous]
\label{lem:ed-goodradius}
Let $Y$ have a law invariant under positive scalar spatial dilation and
suppose it is continuous, starts at $0$, and exits every disk. Then almost
surely
\[
 \tau_{1-\delta}(Y)\uparrow\tau_1(Y),\qquad
 \tau_{1+\delta}(Y)\downarrow\tau_1(Y)
 \quad(\delta\downarrow0).
\]
Consequently the diameter of
$Y[\tau_{1-\delta},\tau_{1+\delta}]$ tends to zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion on the left holds pathwise by continuity. The
nondecreasing function $a\mapsto\tau_a(Y)$ has only countably many
right jumps. Thus the set of radii with
$\tau_{a+}(Y)>\tau_a(Y)$ has Lebesgue measure zero for every sample.
Spatial dilation invariance implies that the probability of such a jump
is the same at every fixed radius. Fubini's theorem on $[1,2]$ makes this
probability zero. Continuity of $Y$ on a compact time interval containing
these exit times then gives the diameter assertion. The argument is
independent of the chosen continuous strictly increasing curve clock.
\end{proof}

For an orientation-preserving real linear map $L$ carrying $\mathbb H$
onto itself, let $\mu_L$ be the law of $L\eta$, where $\eta$ is chordal
SLE$_6$ in $\mathbb H$ from $0$ to $\infty$. Scalar multiples of $L$
give the same law. Normalize
\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}1&u\\0&v\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 (u,v)\in\Theta:=\mathbb R\times(0,\infty).
 \tag{ED1}
\]
The normalized metric represented by this law is
$[(L^{-1})^{\mathsf T}L^{-1}]$.

Choose a countable bounded Lipschitz determining class
$(f_m)_{m\geq1}$ on $\mathcal X$, with values in $[0,1]$ and Lipschitz
constant at most one, allowing constant rescaling of an arbitrary
countable bounded Lipschitz determining class. Such a class can be built
from truncated distances to a countable dense set and finite rational
combinations. Set
\[
 v_m(L)=\mathbb E_{\mu_L}
       f_m(\operatorname{Stop}_1Y).
 \tag{ED2}
\]
The vector $v(L)=(v_m(L))_m$ is Borel in $L$. Indeed, linear image and
first-exit stopping are jointly Borel, so integration against the fixed
ordinary SLE law preserves Borel measurability.

Brownian spatial scaling is mixing: under the Lamperti transform
$Z_s=e^{-s/2}B_{e^s}$ it becomes a shift of a stationary Gaussian process
with covariance $e^{-|s-u|/2}$. Its SLE trace factor, and each fixed
linear image, are therefore dilation mixing. This gives the ergodicity
used below without a separate path-space independence assumption.

The map $L\mapsto v(L)$ is injective. If two unit-radius stopped laws
agreed, scaling invariance would identify their stopped laws at every
radius. Those restrictions determine the entire ordered unbounded curve,
contradicting the half-plane affine identifiability theorem unless the
normalized matrices agree. Therefore the Lusin--Souslin theorem makes
$v(\Theta)$ Borel and its inverse Borel.

Fix $\rho>1$ and $r_n=\rho^{-n}$. For a curve $\gamma$ starting at
$p$ define the local observable
\[
 X_n(\gamma,p)=r_n^{-1}
       \bigl(\gamma[0,\tau_{r_n}(\gamma-p)]-p\bigr).
 \tag{ED3}
\]
If a finite curve fails to exit one of the large initial disks, assign a
cemetery value to that term. It exits all sufficiently small disks, and
finitely many cemetery values do not affect the limits below.
Define
\[
 \ell_m(\gamma,p)=\lim_{N\to\infty}
       \frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N f_m(X_n(\gamma,p)),
 \tag{ED4}
\]
using a cemetery vector if any limit fails to exist. Each limit is a
function of every sufficiently small ordered initial trace prefix:
deleting finitely many summands changes no Ces\`aro limit. In particular
this is a Borel ordered-trace-germ statistic.

\begin{lemma}[The decoder survives a quadratic local error]
\label{lem:ed-linearization}
Let $\eta$ be ordinary half-plane SLE$_6$ and let $\Phi$ be a local
$C^1$ embedding at $0$, defined on a half-neighborhood of $0$, with
\[
 \Phi(0)=0,\qquad \Phi(z)=Lz+O(|z|^2),
 \tag{ED5}
\]
where $L$ is invertible. Assume after a fixed rotation that $L$ maps
$\mathbb H$ onto itself. Let $\gamma$ agree with $\Phi(\eta)$ up to
the first exit from this local chart. Then almost surely
\[
 \ell_m(\gamma,0)=v_m(L)\quad\hbox{for every }m.
 \tag{ED6}
\]
Scalar normalization of $L$ is understood in (ED2).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $Y_n=r_n^{-1}L\eta$. Spatial dilation of the trace includes the
induced capacity reparameterization. Brownian scaling is mixing; its
trace factor and every fixed affine image are therefore dilation
ergodic, as proved in the initial half-plane decoder theorem. Hence
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N
 f_m(\operatorname{Stop}_1Y_n)\longrightarrow v_m(L)
 \quad\hbox{almost surely}.
 \tag{ED7}
\]
One must control the error in this average; pointwise stopping continuity
at all the scaled random samples is not asserted.

For $0<\delta<1/4$ define
\[
 h_\delta(Y)=\min\!\left\{2,
  \operatorname{diam}
   Y[\tau_{1-\delta}(Y),\tau_{1+\delta}(Y)]\right\}.
 \tag{ED8}
\]
The linearization error on a fixed bounded scaled chart is at most
$Cr_n$, uniformly over the sample path until its linear image exits the
disk of radius $1+\delta$. This uniformity is deterministic: the inverse
linear map bounds the relevant preimage disk, and (ED5) bounds the error
there. For all sufficiently large $n$, the first time that
$r_n^{-1}\Phi(\eta)$ reaches radius one lies between the corresponding
$Y_n$ exit times at radii $1-\delta$ and $1+\delta$.
Thus the two stopped curves consist of matched portions with pointwise
error at most $Cr_n$, together with an unmatched terminal portion whose
diameter is bounded by the diameter in (ED8). Increasing
reparameterizations, with pauses allowed, give
\[
 \left|f_m(X_n(\gamma,0))-
 f_m(\operatorname{Stop}_1Y_n)\right|
       \leq Cr_n+h_\delta(Y_n).
 \tag{ED9}
\]
The bounded range of $f_m$ justifies capping the diameter by two.

Take a countable sequence $\delta_k\downarrow0$. Birkhoff's theorem
applied to every $h_{\delta_k}$ gives
\[
 \limsup_N\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N
 \left|f_m(X_n(\gamma,0))-
 f_m(\operatorname{Stop}_1Y_n)\right|
 \leq\mathbb E_{\mu_L}h_{\delta_k}(Y)
 \tag{ED10}
\]
simultaneously for all $m,k$. Lemma~\ref{lem:ed-goodradius} and dominated
convergence make the right side tend to zero as $k\to\infty$. Combine
this with (ED7). Only the initial segment of $\eta$ enters the argument,
since all sufficiently small physical exits occur inside the fixed local
chart. This proves (ED6).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A universal analytic-endpoint tensor decoder]
\label{thm:ed-analytic}
There is a Borel rule, depending only on the ordered initial trace germ,
the endpoint, and its oriented tangent half-plane, that recovers the
projective affine tensor from a single affine-SLE$_6$ sample in every
bounded simply connected marked domain with analytic regular source.
The rest of its boundary and its target may be rough prime-end data.
There is also a terminal version of the rule for every domain with
analytic regular target; the source may then be rough. The rule is the
same for two domains agreeing near that regular endpoint.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let the law be
$(A^{-1})_*\operatorname{SLE}_6(AD,Ap,Aq)$ and assume $p$ is an analytic
regular source. A conformal map $f:\mathbb H\to AD$ with marks
$0,\infty$ extends conformally across $0$ by analytic boundary
reflection, with $f(0)=Ap$ and $f'(0)\ne0$. In a physical tangent frame
$Q$ carrying the interior tangent half-plane to $\mathbb H$, the local
map is
\[
 \Phi(z)=Q(A^{-1}f(z)-p)=Lz+O(|z|^2),
 \qquad L=QA^{-1}M_{f'(0)},
 \tag{ED11}
\]
where $M_c$ denotes multiplication by the nonzero complex number $c$.
The linear map $L$ carries $\mathbb H$ onto itself and has positive
diagonal entries in the boundary frame. Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:ed-linearization}. The Borel inverse of (ED2) recovers
the scalar-normalized $L$, hence the projective tensor
\[
 [(L^{-1})^{\mathsf T}L^{-1}]
       =[QA^{\mathsf T}AQ^{\mathsf T}].
 \tag{ED12}
\]
Conjugating back by the known orthogonal frame $Q$ recovers
$[A^{\mathsf T}A]$. The derivative of the conformal map contributes
only a positive scalar to this tensor. This rule uses the endpoint
germ and tangent half-plane, not the rest of the conformal chart.

Ordinary SLE$_6$ reversibility \cite{ms} says the reversed curve has the same affine-SLE form
with marks swapped. Apply the source decoder to that reversed curve at
the regular target. Reversibility on prime-end domains follows by
conformal transport from the half-plane theorem; only the regular
target germ needs a Euclidean analytic boundary extension.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Terminal singularity and identifiable mixtures]
\label{cor:ed-mixtures}
For one fixed marked domain with analytic regular target, affine-SLE$_6$
laws with distinct projective affine tensors are mutually singular on
the ordered terminal trace-germ sigma-field. More strongly, if
$\pi$ is any Borel probability on normalized affine tensors and
$P_\pi=\int P_T\,\pi(dT)$, then the terminal decoder has law $\pi$
under $P_\pi$. Consequently $\pi\mapsto P_\pi$ is injective, and no
nontrivial affine mixture can equal one pure affine-SLE law.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The same Borel decoder equals $T$ almost surely under $P_T$.
Integrating that equality proves the distribution statement. Distinct
pure laws concentrate on disjoint decoder fibers.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Additional initial-germ obstruction for random mixtures.}
At an analytic regular source, the initial decoder likewise returns the
latent tensor almost surely. Therefore a nontrivial mixture of affine
metrics has a nontrivial initial trace-germ sigma-field. At every
positive exploration time the metric posterior has already collapsed to
a point, even though its prior was nontrivial. This rules out such
mixtures under an additional initial-germ zero-one premise. It does not
rule out a deterministic source-germ metric assignment; that mechanism
still asks whether the current unlabelled frontier recovers the tensor.

\begin{proposition}[DMP forces restarted mixing measures to be pure]
\label{prop:ed-posterior}
Suppose an all-state family has the mixed-affine representation
$P_S=\int\nu^T_S\,\pi_S(dT)$, with Borel mixing kernel $\pi_S$.
Start it in a domain with analytic regular source and target. If it has
strong DMP, then at each admissible stopping time $\tau>0$ before the
target is reached,
\[
 \pi_{S_\tau}=\delta_{D_s(\gamma)}\qquad\hbox{almost surely},
 \tag{ED13}
\]
where $D_s$ is the initial tensor decoder. In particular a rule that
uses a nondegenerate prior at every restarted state cannot satisfy DMP.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Sample the latent tensor $T$ with law $\pi_S$ and then sample the
fixed-$T$ affine SLE. The source decoder gives $D_s(\gamma)=T$ almost
surely, and this variable is measurable from every positive prefix,
including the prefix up to $\tau$. Conditional on this prefix,
ordinary fixed-$T$ SLE DMP therefore gives the pure continuation law
$\nu^{D_s(\gamma)}_{S_\tau}$. The family's DMP gives the mixed law with
mixing measure $\pi_{S_\tau}$. Since $\tau$ precedes reaching the
target, the surviving target end has the same analytic regular germ:
the compact prefix avoids a sufficiently small target neighborhood.
Corollary~\ref{cor:ed-mixtures} applied to this current state makes
the two mixing measures equal, proving (ED13).
\end{proof}



\section{A discrete source shield and stability of two exploration starts}
\label{sec:source-shield}

\paragraph{Status and model.}
The deterministic shielding statement and the ensuing probabilistic estimate
are proved here for independent site percolation on a triangular-lattice
triangulation of a Jordan disk. Boundary vertices are wired black or white,
with exactly two color changes, the marked source and target. The exploration
is the nonbranching face-dual black--white interface between those changes.
The same argument applies to a cellular model with the corresponding
nonbranching primal/dual geometry and the stated white RSW bounds.
It is an actual common-color coupling argument. It is \emph{not} inferred
for an arbitrary random-curve family from abstract locality or G2 alone.

Work at mesh $\delta$. Two triangulated domains, their source positions,
and their boundary colors may differ inside $B(p,\epsilon)$, but agree
outside it. Their common target lies outside $B(p,R_0)$.
Outside the source patch and inside this latter ball, assume the common
geometry is either a lattice half-plane with straight boundary, or a
canonical thin straight slit with its two boundary sides. The lattice
approximations are chosen with free first rows adjacent to the wired
boundary rows; slit width is $O(\delta)$. The black and white wired sides
are the same for the two configurations outside the patch.
Couple all free colors outside the patch identically. Those shared colors
have the independent percolation law. The colors inside the patch may be
coupled arbitrarily, with their correct marginal laws.

\subsection{The deterministic one-edge crosscut}

In a triangular face, either zero or two of its primal edges have endpoints
of different colors. Hence the dual interface graph has degree zero or two
at every interior dual vertex. In the Dobrushin disk it has a unique open
simple path between the source and target; other components are loops.

\begin{lemma}[A one-heterochromatic-edge shield]
\label{lem:ss-oneedge}
Let a simple primal crosscut $c$ of a triangulated disk connect a white
wired boundary vertex to a black wired boundary vertex. Suppose every
edge of $c$ is white--white except its final edge $e$, which joins a white
\emph{interior} vertex to the black boundary vertex. Suppose $c$ separates
the entire source patch from the target. Then the exploration must use
the dual edge $e^*$, with the direction determined by its black and white
sides. After using $e^*$ it cannot reenter the source side of the crosscut.

Consequently, if the same such crosscut and its colors occur in both
configurations above, their explorations have identical ordered suffixes
from $e^*$ to the common target.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The source--target dual path must cross the primal crosscut. Primal and
dual vertices are distinct, and dual edges cross primal edges only at
their matched transverse intersections. No interface edge can cross a
white--white primal edge. Thus the crossing must be $e^*$, and matching
colors give the same direction in both configurations.

The dual interface path is simple, so it cannot traverse $e^*$ twice.
All other primal edges of $c$ remain monochromatic, so they cannot be
crossed later either. The path therefore stays on the target side after
using $e^*$. In particular it never returns to the altered source patch.
Outside the patch the two triangulations and all colors coincide.
At each subsequent dual vertex the outgoing edge is uniquely determined
by the local colors and the incoming edge. Starting at the same directed
edge $e^*$, the two paths consequently have the same entire suffix.
\end{proof}

Using boundary \emph{vertices} in this formulation avoids an ambiguity of
cell-exterior pictures: $e$ is an interior primal edge incident to a boundary
vertex, so its dual edge is an ordinary interior edge. Neither a crossing
through a dual/primal common vertex nor an unexplained passage around the
end of a physical boundary edge is being used.

\subsection{Uniform probability of a white arch}

Assume a uniform white RSW lower bound for fixed-aspect rectangles of free
vertices. No connection to a prescribed individual vertex is assumed.
We give the geometry for a horizontal half-plane source, translated to $p=0$,
with black boundary on the negative real side and white boundary on the
positive side. Let $r\gg\epsilon+\delta$.

Use a white vertical crossing in each of
\[
 [-3r,-2r]\times[0,3r],\qquad [2r,3r]\times[0,3r],
\]
and a white horizontal crossing of
\[
 [-3r,3r]\times[r,2r].
\]
At the bottom of a stem, start the rectangle in the first free vertex
row, not in the fixed wired row. Rounding by $O(\delta)$ changes the
aspect ratios by a uniformly bounded amount. The crossings in the
overlapping squares intersect by planarity. Thus their union contains a
white path from a free vertex adjacent to the black boundary to a free
vertex adjacent to the white boundary. Add a white--white edge at the
white endpoint and choose an edge $e$ to a black boundary vertex at the
other endpoint. Trim the white path at its last white-wired-boundary contact before reaching the left endpoint, so that all its subsequent white vertices are interior; then erase loops. This gives a proper simple primal crosscut with just the one heterochromatic edge $e$.

All these vertices lie, allowing lattice rounding, in
$A(0,r/2,6r)$. The crosscut and the boundary arc through the source
enclose the source patch. Its source-side component is inside $B(0,6r)$:
the crosscut and that local boundary arc form a Jordan polygon contained
there, so their bounded interior is also contained there. The common
half-annulus between the patch and crosscut shows that the entire altered
domain portion of the source patch is on that side.

Each rectangle event is increasing in white colors of independent free
vertices. RSW and FKG therefore give a constant $\vartheta>0$, independent
of $r,\delta$ and the contents of the source patch, for this white-arch
event. Importantly, the left endpoint is \emph{some} first-row white vertex
adjacent to the black boundary, selected after the crossing occurs. Requiring
connection to one predetermined microscopic vertex would not have this
uniform lower bound.

\paragraph{Straight slit geometry.}
The same construction works for a finite-width lattice slit along the
negative real axis, with black upper lip and white lower lip, by using
five crossings around a square annulus: an upper-left vertical stem,
a top horizontal crossing, a right vertical crossing, a bottom horizontal
crossing, and a lower-left vertical stem. The stems occupy
$[-3r,-2r]\times[0,3r]$ and
$[-3r,-2r]\times[-3r,0]$ on their respective sides of the slit.
The other three rectangles may be
\[
 [-3r,3r]\times[2r,3r],\quad
 [2r,3r]\times[-3r,3r],\quad
 [-3r,3r]\times[-3r,-2r].
\]
The first free upper row is adjacent to the black wired lip; the first
free lower row is adjacent to the white wired lip. The resulting crosscut
goes around the right side of the slit and separates its tip patch from
the distant target. The boundary arc through the tip, including the two
separate lattice lips, stays inside $B(0,6r)$. The finite-width triangulated
domain is Jordan; one need not identify its two physical boundary rows.
RSW and FKG again give a uniform $\vartheta>0$ after adjusting constants.

\subsection{Polynomial source-patch stability}

\begin{theorem}[Common-color coalescence outside a source patch]
\label{thm:ss-coalescence}
Under the preceding geometric and percolation hypotheses, there are
constants $C<\infty$ and $\alpha>0$ depending only on the RSW bounds and
the fixed geometry such that, for $0<R<R_0/6$, the common-color coupling
of the two full exploration curves satisfies
\[
 \mathbb P\{d_{\mathrm F}(\gamma_1,\gamma_2)>2R\}
       \leq C\left(\frac{\epsilon+\delta}{R}\right)^\alpha.
 \tag{SH1}
\]
More concretely, outside an event of the displayed probability the curves
coalesce at a directed dual edge inside $B(p,R)$; both prefixes before
that edge stay inside this ball, and the entire suffixes are identical.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $r_0=16(\epsilon+\delta)$ and $r_j=16^j r_0$.
For every $j$ with $6r_j\leq R$, the corresponding white-arch event
depends only on shared free vertices in
$A(p,r_j/2,6r_j)$. These vertex sets are disjoint for different $j$,
up to an immaterial lattice enlargement absorbed by increasing $r_0$.
The events are therefore independent and each has probability at least
$\vartheta$. If there are $N$ available bands, the probability of no
shield is at most $(1-\vartheta)^N$.

On any shield event select a white crosscut by a deterministic finite-graph
rule using only shared colors, for example a lexicographic simple path in
the crossing cluster. The two configurations select the same crosscut and
the same single heterochromatic edge. Lemma~\ref{lem:ss-oneedge} gives a
common suffix. Before crossing the shield, each path stays in its
source-side component, contained in $B(p,6r_j)\subset B(p,R)$.
Synchronize the two prefixes by any increasing reparameterization and then
align their common suffix. The prefixes are in a ball of diameter $2R$,
so their oriented Fr\'echet distance is at most $2R$.

The number of bands is a logarithmic function of $R/(\epsilon+\delta)$.
For instance with
$\alpha=-\log(1-\vartheta)/\log16>0$, enlarging a fixed constant $C$
gives $(1-\vartheta)^N\leq
C((\epsilon+\delta)/R)^\alpha$. This also covers the case with no
available band by making its right-hand side at least one.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Passage to marginal curve limits.}
Suppose along a common mesh subsequence the two exploration laws converge
in the usual Polish oriented uniform-Fr\'echet curve space. The coupled
pairs are tight because their two marginals are tight. Choose a joint
subsequential limit. The set
$\{d_{\mathrm F}>2R\}$ is open in the product curve space; the Portmanteau
inequality transfers (SH1), with $\delta\downarrow0$, to this limiting
coupling. Thus the same quantitative source-patch stability holds for the
two marginal curve limits. It requires neither convergence of random
slit-domain kernels nor a limiting strong DMP theorem.

If source patches themselves shrink and the mesh is negligible relative
to their radii, this gives continuity in law for these particular local
source perturbations. One may first fix $R$ and let $\epsilon\downarrow0$,
then let $R\downarrow0$, or balance the coupling estimate to obtain an
explicit Prokhorov rate.

\paragraph{What is not proved.}
This is not a consequence of abstract unforced-crossing estimates for one
curve. It uses a common independent coloring and the planar nonbranching
interface to force \emph{coalescence at one edge}. It also is not the full
marked inverse-conformal-chart stability condition used in the affine-ansatz
support theorems: arbitrary converging charts may create forced thin fjords,
noncommon microscopic boundaries, or source approaches outside this shared
half-plane/slit geometry. Extending the shield coupling to such approximations
requires additional work. No classification of arbitrary source-local
exploration ensembles follows from (SH1) alone.

\section{Exact suffix coalescence survives curve limits}
\label{sec:coalescence-bridge}

The source-shield estimate contains more information than a small
Fr\'echet distance. Its exact common suffix is stable under taking a
joint curve limit, provided that the prefixes stay in a fixed closed set
away from the target. This allows the discontinuous terminal metric
decoder to be used without asserting that it is a continuous observable.

Let $K\subset\mathbb C$ be closed and consider two oriented continuous
curves. Define $E_K$ to be the relation
that there exist cut times $s_1,s_2$ in continuous representatives on
$[0,1]$ such that
\[
 \gamma_i[0,s_i]\subset K\quad(i=1,2),\qquad
 \gamma_1[s_1,1]=\gamma_2[s_2,1]
 \quad\text{as ordered unparameterized curves}.
 \tag{CB1}
\]
The relation is independent of the chosen representatives and allows
pauses, as does the usual uniform Fr\'echet curve space. It implies a
common endpoint, which may belong to $K$ in this definition. All
applications below have limiting common target $q\notin K$.

\begin{lemma}[A closed relation of common ordered suffixes]
\label{lem:cb-closed}
$E_K$ is closed in the product uniform-Fr\'echet curve space.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $(\gamma_1^n,\gamma_2^n)\to(\gamma_1,\gamma_2)$ and the pairs
belong to $E_K$. Choose representatives of each convergent sequence
converging uniformly to fixed continuous representatives of its limit.
This is possible by the definition of the Fr\'echet metric; reparameterize
the cut times along with the curves. Pass to a subsequence on which both
cut times $s_i^n$ converge to $s_i\in[0,1]$.

Closedness of $K$ and uniform convergence give
$\gamma_i[0,s_i]\subset K$. Linear rescaling of $[s_i^n,1]$ to $[0,1]$ makes
the restricted curves converge uniformly to the restrictions on
$[s_i,1]$, by uniform continuity of $\gamma_i$ (a cut at $s_i=1$
gives a constant curve). The two restrictions
at stage $n$ have Fr\'echet distance zero. Their limits therefore also
have distance zero and are the same ordered curve. Thus the limiting
pair belongs to $E_K$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Coalescence transfer and comparison of metric priors]
\label{prop:cb-priors}
Suppose pairs of discrete curves with a common target converge marginally
to laws $P_1,P_2$ on the oriented curve space, and their couplings satisfy
\[
 \mathbb P(E_K)\ge1-e_n,\qquad \limsup_n e_n\le e<1,
 \tag{CB2}
\]
where $K$ is a fixed closed ball not containing the target. Each limiting
domain is analytic and regular near that target, and the two colored
target germs agree. Suppose
\[
 P_i=\int P_{i,T}\,\pi_i(dT),
 \tag{CB3}
\]
where $P_{i,T}$ is the affine-SLE$_6$ law with normalized tensor $T$ in
domain $i$, and $\pi_i$ are Borel probability measures on tensors.
Then, with total variation defined as $\sup_B|\pi_1(B)-\pi_2(B)|$,
\[
 \|\pi_1-\pi_2\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le e.
 \tag{CB4}
\]
In particular if both marginals are pure affine-SLE laws, any positive
uniform lower bound on the discrete coalescence probability forces their
tensors to be equal.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Tightness of the marginals makes the coupled pairs tight. Take a joint
subsequential limit. Lemma~\ref{lem:cb-closed} and the closed-set
Portmanteau inequality give $\mathbb P(E_K)\ge1-e$ in the limit.
Because the target is outside $K$, the common suffix on $E_K$ includes
a nonconstant ordered terminal germ at that target. The universal
terminal decoder of Theorem~\ref{thm:ed-analytic} is the same for the
two domains, since their colored target germs agree. Let $T_i$ be its
value on curve $i$. The laws of these random variables are $\pi_i$,
and on $E_K$ they are equal. Hence
\[
 \sup_B|\mathbb P(T_1\in B)-\mathbb P(T_2\in B)|
       \le\mathbb P(T_1\ne T_2)\le e.
\]
For two distinct point masses the left-hand side equals one, proving the
last assertion. The proof uses exact suffix equality, not continuity of
the decoder and not just small curve distance.
\end{proof}

For the source-shield coupling, take $K=\overline B(p,R)$ with the
regular target outside this ball. Common discrete targets may vary with
mesh, provided $q_n\to q\notin K$; the closed-relation proof already allows
this. Equation (SH1) then gives
\[
 \|\pi_1-\pi_2\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
   \le C(\epsilon/R)^\alpha
 \tag{CB5}
\]
for two source-patch perturbations with common flat or straight-slit
annuli. This is a quantitative stability statement for mixing measures,
and yields exact tensor equality for pure marginals once the right side
is less than one. It is conditional on the affine-mixture marginal
ansatz; no such ansatz is derived from RSW here.


\subsection{Stopping an ordered unparameterized curve on a circle}

Fix $p\in\mathbb R^2$ and $R_0>0$. Work in the usual metric space of oriented
continuous curves modulo increasing reparameterization, with the uniform
Fr\'echet topology. Curves start at $p$ and end outside
$\overline{B(p,R_0)}$. Small changes of the starting points are harmless;
for the sequence version they need only lie inside every fixed smaller ball
for sufficiently large indices.
For a continuous representative $\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb R^2$ and
$0<r<R_0$, put
\[
 \tau_\gamma(r)=\inf\{t:|\gamma(t)-p|\geq r\},\qquad
 E_r\gamma=\gamma|_{[0,\tau_\gamma(r)]}.
 \tag{LT1}
\]
The stopped path is regarded as an ordered unparameterized curve, so this
definition is independent of pauses or changes of clock.

\begin{lemma}[Almost every circle is a continuity circle]
\label{lem:lt-stopcontinuity}
For each fixed continuous curve $\gamma$, the map
$(\eta,s)\mapsto E_s\eta$ is continuous at $(\gamma,r)$ for all but at most
countably many radii $r\in(0,R_0)$. The stopping map is Borel.
Consequently, for any probability law $\mu$ on these curves, $E_r$ is
$\mu$-almost surely continuous for Lebesgue-almost every deterministic $r$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose any continuous representative of $\gamma$.
The function $r\mapsto\tau_\gamma(r)$ is nondecreasing and hence has at
most countably many discontinuities. Suppose it is continuous at $r$.
If $(\gamma_n,r_n)\to(\gamma,r)$ in the product of Fr\'echet and ordinary
metrics, choose increasing reparameterizations for which representatives
$\widetilde\gamma_n$ converge uniformly to $\gamma$.
Writing $\varepsilon_n=\|\widetilde\gamma_n-\gamma\|_\infty+|r_n-r|$,
first-hit times obey, for every slightly larger positive error tending to zero,
\[
 \tau_\gamma(r-2\varepsilon_n)
 \ \leq\ \tau_{\widetilde\gamma_n}(r_n)
 \ \leq\ \tau_\gamma(r+2\varepsilon_n).
 \tag{LT2}
\]
If $\varepsilon_n=0$ the assertion is immediate; using a strictly larger error
avoids any immaterial equality convention. Continuity of $\tau_\gamma$ at
$r$ gives convergence of the middle times to $\tau_\gamma(r)$.
Uniform continuity of $\gamma$ then makes the stopped representatives,
linearly recoded onto $[0,1]$, converge uniformly. This proves joint
continuity at $(\gamma,r)$.

The stopping map is understood on all curves reaching the selected radius, including already stopped curves reaching a larger radius. For parameterized curves the stopping time is Borel, since
\[
 \{\tau_\gamma(r)\leq t\}
 =\left\{\max_{0\leq u\leq t}|\gamma(u)-p|\geq r\right\}.
\]
Stopping and linear recoding are therefore Borel path operations. They
commute with reparameterization and give the usual Borel stopping map on
ordered unparameterized curves.

For completeness, the almost-every-radius assertion does not require a
measurable choice of representatives. For a map between metric spaces its
set of continuity points is a $G_\delta$ set, obtained from its oscillations
on neighborhoods. Apply this to the joint stopping map. Its discontinuity
set in the product of curve space and radius space is Borel, and every
vertical section has Lebesgue measure zero by the countability argument
above. Fubini's theorem now gives the assertion under $\mu$.
\end{proof}


\section{Independent color resampling, initial germs, and a common affine metric}
\label{sec:affine-mixture-purity}


\begin{theorem}[Initial-germ zero-one law from independent colors and shielding]
\label{thm:ed-germzeroone}
Let $P$ be a subsequential exploration-curve limit of the independent
color model in a fixed domain with flat source germ. Assume the Polish
oriented Fr\'echet curve topology and the flat-source shield estimate of
Theorem~\ref{thm:ss-coalescence}. Then the initial ordered trace-germ
sigma-field is $P$-trivial. Precisely, if $\sigma_r\gamma$ denotes the
ordered prefix stopped on the radius-$r$ circle about the source, this
field is $\bigcap_{r>0}\overline{\sigma(\sigma_r\gamma)}^{\,P}$.
It is the usual ordered positive-prefix germ, with no clock chosen.
No affine ansatz, terminal tensor decoder,
limiting DMP theorem, or continuum configuration-measurability theorem
is required.

More quantitatively, for each sufficiently small $r$, every bounded
observable $F$ of the prefix stopped at radius $r$ and every bounded
Lipschitz observable $G$ of the entire curve satisfy
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_P(FG)-\mathbb E_PF\,\mathbb E_PG\right|
 \leq C\|F\|_\infty
   (\operatorname{Lip}G+\|G\|_\infty)r^{\alpha/(1+\alpha)},
 \tag{EDG}
\]
first at almost every stopping-continuity radius $r$. Constants and
length units are fixed by the source geometry. The zero-one assertion
itself holds on the full initial-germ sigma-field.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a source-patch radius $\epsilon$ and couple two discrete
configurations with independent colors inside $B(p,\epsilon)$ and shared
independent colors outside. For any fixed $r<\epsilon/3$ and all small
enough meshes, the first exploration stopped at radius $r$ is determined
entirely by its own interior colors. It is therefore independent of the
\emph{entire second exploration}, which uses only the shared outer colors
and the second interior color set.

Take a joint subsequential curve limit. Both marginals equal $P$. Choose
an almost-sure stopping-continuity radius $r<\epsilon/3$. The continuous
mapping theorem preserves the product law, so the stopped prefix of
$\gamma_1$ is independent of the full curve $\gamma_2$. Consequently
any bounded initial-germ observable $F(\gamma_1)$ is independent of
$G(\gamma_2)$ for every bounded Lipschitz $G$ on the full curve space.
This step concerns the limiting product law; it does not attempt to pass
an arbitrary discontinuous germ statistic through a discrete limit.

The shield estimate supplies, in this same coupling, for a fixed small
$R$ and $\epsilon\ll R$,
\[
 \mathbb P\{d_{\mathrm F}(\gamma_1,\gamma_2)>2R\}
       \leq C(\epsilon/R)^\alpha.
\]
Thus
\begin{align*}
 \left|\mathbb E_P(FG)-\mathbb E_PF\,\mathbb E_PG\right|
 &=\left|\mathbb E
       F(\gamma_1)(G(\gamma_1)-G(\gamma_2))\right|\\
 &\leq\|F\|_\infty
   \left(2\operatorname{Lip}(G)R+
           2C\|G\|_\infty(\epsilon/R)^\alpha\right).
 \tag{EDG1}
\end{align*}
The left side depends only on the marginal law $P$. Let
$\epsilon\downarrow0$ with $R$ fixed and then $R\downarrow0$.
It vanishes. Bounded Lipschitz functions determine finite signed
measures on a Polish space, so every bounded initial-germ observable
is independent of the full curve sigma-field. Since it belongs to
that sigma-field, it is almost surely constant. This proves initial
trace-germ triviality, including its completion.

For the quantitative prefix assertion, start with a fixed good radius
$r$, use $\epsilon=4r$, and take
$R=r^{\alpha/(1+\alpha)}$ after fixing the reference length unit.
For sufficiently small $r$ this is an admissible radius with
$\epsilon\ll R$. Formula (EDG1) gives (EDG).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Pure affine limit at a flat source]
\label{prop:ed-resampling}
Let one independent site-percolation model satisfy the flat-source shield
coupling of Theorem~\ref{thm:ss-coalescence}. Let $D$ have a flat source
germ. Suppose along one mesh subsequence its
exploration converges to
\[
 P=\int \nu^T_{D,p,q}\,\pi(dT),
 \tag{ED14}
\]
where $\pi$ is a Borel probability on projective affine tensors. Assume
the standard Polish oriented curve topology. Then $\pi$ is a point mass.
No limiting domain Markov theorem or continuum configuration-measurability
theorem is needed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The regular source endpoint decoder has distribution $\pi$ under (ED14)
and is measurable in the initial trace-germ sigma-field. This sigma-field
is trivial by Theorem~\ref{thm:ed-germzeroone}. Thus the decoder is almost
surely one deterministic tensor, and $\pi$ is a point mass. The target
need only meet the curve-convergence assumptions here; analytic target
regularity enters the source-comparison theorem below.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Splicing a regular colored source germ]
\label{lem:ed-splicing}
Fix a bounded reference domain $D_0$ with flat regular source $p=0$,
analytic regular target $q$, and one fixed boundary-color convention.
Given any analytic regular colored source germ $G$ at $0$ with that
convention, and any sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$, there is a bounded
marked domain $D_{G,\epsilon}$ agreeing with $D_0$ outside
$B(0,\epsilon)$ and having exactly the germ $G$ at $0$. Its target
germ at $q$ agrees with $D_0$. The boundary can be chosen smooth except
for any corners already present in $D_0$; only its two marked germs
need to be analytic.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For any analytic regular colored source germ $G$ at $p$ and any small
$\epsilon$, an orientation-preserving smooth diffeomorphism supported in
$B(p,\epsilon)$ can map the source germ of one fixed flat-source
reference domain to $G$, fixing $p$ and the distant regular target.
For an explicit local construction, use a radial twist to rotate a small
core to the required tangent, followed by a cutoff graph shear to realize
the analytic curvature in a still smaller core. The graph derivative can
be made arbitrarily small, so the cutoff shear remains a diffeomorphism.
Call the resulting domain $D_{G,\epsilon}$. It agrees with the reference
outside the source patch and agrees with $G$ in a still smaller neighborhood.
The support avoids $q$, and ambient orientation preserves the boundary
color order. Applying the diffeomorphism to $D_0$ proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[One deterministic metric from a coherent affine mixture]
\label{thm:ed-coherent}
Consider a translation-covariant source-local exploration family arising
from one independent Bernoulli nonbranching cellular percolation model
admitting the flat-source shield coupling of
Theorem~\ref{thm:ss-coalescence}. Suppose each bounded-domain marginal
with analytic regular marks is a Borel mixture of fixed affine-SLE$_6$
laws. Require the family to include the source splices of
Lemma~\ref{lem:ed-splicing}, and require coherent subsequential limits
for these comparisons: for every such splice and the reference, compatible
common-color discrete approximations along one common mesh subsequence
realize the two specified marginal limits.

Then one deterministic projective affine tensor is shared by every
such marginal, and each marginal is the pure affine-SLE$_6$ law for
that tensor. Strong DMP and marked inverse-chart continuity are not
premises of this theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The reference mixing measure $\pi_0$ is a point mass by
Proposition~\ref{prop:ed-resampling}. Fix another original domain with
analytic regular source germ $G$. Translation covariance allows its source
to be placed at $0$ without changing its physical projective tensor.
For every small $\epsilon$, source locality identifies the original
initial prefix law with that in $D_{G,\epsilon}$ through a common source
neighborhood. The source tensor decoder is a function of this prefix
and the common germ. Its distribution is the mixing measure in either
state, so both have one common mixing measure $\pi_G$, independent of
$\epsilon$.

Coherence and the common-color shield coupling give a coupling of the
two limiting curves in $D_{G,\epsilon}$ and $D_0$ such that their ordered
terminal segments agree outside an event of probability at most
$C(\epsilon/R)^\alpha$, for one fixed small $R$ in the shared flat
outer geometry. Their target germs coincide. Apply the two terminal
decoders; the resulting tensor variables have respective laws
$\pi_G,\pi_0$ and are equal on the coalescence event. The coupling
inequality for total variation therefore gives
\[
 \|\pi_G-\pi_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
       \leq C(\epsilon/R)^\alpha.
 \tag{ED16}
\]
Here total variation is $\sup_B|\pi_G(B)-\pi_0(B)|$.
Letting $\epsilon\downarrow0$ gives $\pi_G=\pi_0$. Since $\pi_0$ is
a point mass, the original marginal is pure and shares the same tensor.
The argument applies separately to each original regular-source state;
no simultaneous uncountable full-probability assertion is required.
\end{proof}

Thus, subject to the explicitly stated coherent independent-color scaling
and shielding premises, the mixed-affine ansatz on regular domains
already collapses to one deterministic common affine metric. This does
not derive the mixed-affine ansatz for an arbitrary curve law, and does
not classify arbitrary abstract three-axiom families.

\paragraph{Initial characteristic limits become deterministic.}
At any source to which the preceding zero-one argument applies, a finite
initial limit in probability of an adapted characteristic $A_t/t$ or
$C_t/t$ is deterministic. To see germ measurability, stop the characteristic
at the first exit from an arbitrary source ball. That exit has strictly
positive capacity time; the stopped and unstopped limits therefore agree.
A limit in probability of variables measurable from the stopped prefix has
a measurable version in its completed sigma-field. This holds for every
small radius, so the limit belongs to the trivial initial germ.
This removes the deterministic-value premise at these starts once existence
of a tangent and natural-trace-filtration compatibility are known; neither
tangent existence nor the required slit-uniform bound is obtained here.



\section{Total-variation decoupling of an initial prefix and a distant suffix}
\label{sec:spatial-beta-mixing}

The closed exact-coalescence relation gives a stronger consequence than
bounded-Lipschitz covariance. It controls arbitrary measurable observables
of the distant terminal suffix in total variation. This uses the actual
independent-color source-shield coupling, not just abstract locality or
an unforced-crossing bound.

Fix a bounded domain with source $p$, target $q$, flat source geometry,
and a marginal curve scaling limit $P$ satisfying the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:ed-germzeroone}. Let $\sigma_r\gamma$ be the initial
ordered prefix stopped on the radius-$r$ circle about $p$. For
$R<|q-p|$ define $\lambda_R\gamma$ to be the ordered terminal suffix
beginning at the \emph{last} visit to $\overline B(p,R)$. For any
continuous representative on $[0,1]$ this means restriction from
\[
 t_R(\gamma)=\max\{t\in[0,1]:|\gamma(t)-p|\leq R\}
\]
to $1$, followed by linear reparameterization. The resulting ordered
curve does not depend on the representative or pauses.

Both stopping operations are Borel maps on the assumed Polish oriented
Fr\'echet curve space. For completeness, on the continuous-parameter
curve space, $t_R$ is Borel because, for fixed $a$,
\[
 \{t_R\geq a\}
 =\left\{\min_{a\leq t\leq1}|\gamma(t)-p|\leq R\right\}
\]
is closed. Restriction and rescaling are jointly continuous in a curve
and its cut parameter. Hence the terminal-suffix map to the quotient
curve space is Borel on representatives and invariant under
reparameterization. It descends to a Borel map on the Polish quotient:
the preimages of Borel sets are saturated Borel sets, whose images and
complements under the continuous quotient map are analytic; both are
therefore Borel. First-exit stopping has the same Borel property.

\begin{theorem}[Prefix--suffix decoupling]
\label{thm:spatial-beta}
There exist $C<\infty$ and $\alpha>0$, determined by the source-shield
constants, such that for all sufficiently small $R$ and all
$0<r<R/64$,
\[
 \left\|\mathcal L_P(\sigma_r\gamma,\lambda_R\gamma)
       -\mathcal L_P(\sigma_r\gamma)
           \otimes\mathcal L_P(\lambda_R\gamma)\right\|_{\rm TV}
 \leq C(r/R)^\alpha.
 \tag{SB1}
\]
Here total variation is $\sup_A|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|$ on the product
space. In particular this estimate holds at \emph{every} small radius
$r$, not merely almost every stopping-continuity radius.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set $\epsilon=8r$. At the discrete level take two configurations with
independent colors in $B(p,\epsilon)$ and identical independent colors
outside that ball. Both marginal explorations have the same scaling
limit $P$. Their joint laws are tight. Choose a radius
$\rho\in(r,2r)$ at which first-exit stopping is $P$-almost surely
continuous; such radii have full Lebesgue measure by
Lemma~\ref{lem:lt-stopcontinuity}.
The first discrete exploration up to radius $\rho$ uses only the first
configuration's interior colors, once the mesh is sufficiently small.
It is consequently independent of the entire second exploration.
Continuous mapping along a joint subsequential limit gives
\[
 \sigma_\rho\gamma_1\ \perp\!\!\!\perp\ \gamma_2.
\]
Stopping the limiting first prefix again, now at radius $r$, is a Borel
operation and requires no continuity assertion at $r$. Thus
$\sigma_r\gamma_1$ is independent of the full curve $\gamma_2$.

Apply the source-shield estimate with shield radius $R/2$. Outside an
event of probability at most $C(r/R)^\alpha$, both prefixes before
coalescence stay in $K=\overline B(p,R/2)$ and the ordered suffixes
are identical. Lemma~\ref{lem:cb-closed} and the closed-set Portmanteau
inequality preserve this statement in the same joint subsequential
limit. Since $q\notin\overline B(p,R)$, each full curve's last visit
to $\overline B(p,R)$ is on the common suffix. Therefore
\[
 \mathbb P\{\lambda_R\gamma_1\ne\lambda_R\gamma_2\}
 \leq C(r/R)^\alpha.
\]
The pairs
$(\sigma_r\gamma_1,\lambda_R\gamma_1)$ and
$(\sigma_r\gamma_1,\lambda_R\gamma_2)$ form a coupling of the
joint law in (SB1) with the displayed product law. The usual coupling
bound for total variation proves (SB1).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Expected conditional total-variation stability]
\label{cor:spatial-conditional-tv}
Let $K_r(x,\cdot)$ be a regular conditional law of $\lambda_R\gamma$
given $\sigma_r\gamma=x$. Then
\[
 \int\left\|K_r(x,\cdot)-\mathcal L_P(\lambda_R\gamma)
          \right\|_{\rm TV}
       \,\mathcal L_P(\sigma_r\gamma)(dx)
 \leq C(r/R)^\alpha.
 \tag{SB2}
\]
Consequently for bounded Borel functions $F,G$ of the two respective
variables,
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}_P(F(\sigma_r\gamma),G(\lambda_R\gamma))|
 \leq 2C\|F\|_\infty\|G\|_\infty(r/R)^\alpha.
 \tag{SB3}
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
On standard Borel spaces, total variation between a joint law and the
product of its marginals is the integral of the total variations of
the corresponding regular conditional laws. One may verify the identity
using a countable generating algebra to compute the variation of each
signed conditional measure. This gives (SB2). Integrating the bounded
product observable against the difference of the two probability laws
costs at most twice its supremum norm times the convention for total
variation used in (SB1), proving (SB3).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Variants and limitation.}
The same proof applies to any standard-Borel-valued observable $Y_R$
that agrees whenever two curves belong to $E_{\overline B(p,R/2)}$.
For example one may use the compact trace outside the open radius-$R$
ball instead of the ordered distant suffix. Measurability of a given
variant should be checked separately. Completed prefix sigma-fields
are harmless, since versions may be chosen modulo their common
marginal null sets.

This is quantitative spatial loss of memory at a deterministic regular
source. It does not identify a conditional continuation kernel in an
arbitrary random slit domain: the proof has no shield estimate uniform
over rough explored frontiers. In particular it neither proves strong
DMP in a curve limit nor the whole-slit stability premise of the
driving-rigidity results. The whole-tube approach based on boundary
three-arm estimates still needs the deterministic implication from
ordered curve disagreement to an appropriate arm event.


\section{Annular crossing statistics self-average at a regular source}
\label{sec:annular-scenery}

The same source resampling argument applies to a macroscopic observable
whenever the closed exact-coalescence relation forces its equality. It
therefore controls annular crossings made \emph{before the first outer
exit}, even if the full curve later returns arbitrarily close to the
source. No source nonreturn assumption is needed.

\begin{proposition}[Decoupling for coalescence-invariant observations]
\label{prop:scenery-decoupling}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:spatial-beta}, let $Y_R$ be
a Borel map from the oriented curve space into a standard Borel space.
Assume
\[
 (\gamma,\widetilde\gamma)\in E_{\overline B(p,R/2)}
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 Y_R(\gamma)=Y_R(\widetilde\gamma).
 \tag{AS1}
\]
Then, for $0<r<R/64$,
\[
 \bigl\|\mathcal L_P(\sigma_r\gamma,Y_R(\gamma))
      -\mathcal L_P(\sigma_r\gamma)
                    \otimes\mathcal L_P(Y_R(\gamma))\bigr\|_{\rm TV}
 \le C(r/R)^\alpha.
 \tag{AS2}
\]
The same conclusion holds if the first variable is any Borel function
of $\sigma_r\gamma$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use exactly the independent-inner/shared-outer coupling in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:spatial-beta}, including a good stopping-continuity
radius between $r$ and $2r$. Its limiting curves obey
$\sigma_r\gamma_1\perp\!\!\!\perp\gamma_2$ and
\[
 \mathbb P\bigl(E_{\overline B(p,R/2)}\bigr)
       \ge1-C(r/R)^\alpha.
\]
Assumption (AS1) implies
$\mathbb P\{Y_R(\gamma_1)\ne Y_R(\gamma_2)\}
\le C(r/R)^\alpha$. Couple the two pairs with common first variable
$\sigma_r\gamma_1$, as in the earlier proof. The second pair has the
product law by independence. This proves (AS2). Applying a Borel map
to the first variable cannot increase total variation.
\end{proof}

Fix $a>0$ sufficiently small that the source-shield construction applies
up to scale $a$, and $|q-p|>a$. Put
\[
 r_n=a2^{-n},\qquad n=1,2,\ldots.
\]
For a continuous representative of $\gamma$ define
\[
 \tau_n=\inf\{t:|\gamma(t)-p|=2r_n\},\qquad
 \eta_n=\max\{t\le\tau_n:|\gamma(t)-p|\le r_n\}.
 \tag{AS3}
\]
Let $B_n(\gamma)$ be the ordered restriction to $[\eta_n,\tau_n]$,
translated by $-p$ and dilated by $r_n^{-1}$. This is an oriented curve
in $\{1\le |z|\le2\}$ from its inner to its outer boundary. It is the
last inner-to-outer crossing before the first outer hit. Only this
crossing is recorded; later returns of the full curve are immaterial.

The map $B_n$ is Borel and invariant under changes of representative.
Indeed $\tau_n$ is a Borel first-hitting operation. Restrict first to
$[0,\tau_n]$, linearly reparameterized on $[0,1]$. On this stopped
curve, $\eta_n$ is a Borel last visit to the closed inner ball, by the
last-visit argument in Section~\ref{sec:spatial-beta-mixing}. Successive
restriction, translation, dilation, and passage to the oriented quotient
are Borel. In particular $B_n$ is a Borel function of
$\sigma_{2r_n}\gamma$.

There is a second property crucial to the proof: if
$(\gamma,\widetilde\gamma)\in E_{\overline B(p,r_n/2)}$, then
$B_n(\gamma)=B_n(\widetilde\gamma)$. The prefixes before the common
suffix remain strictly inside the inner circle, and the common suffix
starts there. Both first outer hits thus occur on that suffix, and so
do their last inner-ball visits before those hits. The corresponding
ordered crossing segments agree exactly.

\begin{theorem}[Exponential absolute regularity across dyadic source scales]
\label{thm:scenery-beta}
Under the same source-shield and independent-color limit hypotheses,
the annular crossing sequence $(B_n)_{n\ge1}$ satisfies
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\bigl\|\mathcal L_P\bigl((B_1,\ldots,B_m),
                                  (B_{m+k},B_{m+k+1},\ldots)\bigr)\\
 &\hspace{14mm}
 -\mathcal L_P(B_1,\ldots,B_m)
             \otimes\mathcal L_P(B_{m+k},B_{m+k+1},\ldots)
                         \bigr\|_{\rm TV}
 \le C_0 2^{-\alpha k},\qquad m,k\ge1.
 \end{split}
 \tag{AS4}
\]
The countable product on the fine-scale side is a standard Borel space.
Constants are independent of $m$ and $k$. No stationarity or dilation
covariance of the marginal curve limit is assumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $k\ge8$, apply Proposition~\ref{prop:scenery-decoupling} with
$R=r_m$ and $r=2r_{m+k}$. The vector
$Y_R=(B_1,\ldots,B_m)$ satisfies (AS1): every one of its inner
radii is at least $r_m$, so coalescence inside the radius-$r_m/2$
ball occurs before all the relevant inner and outer visits. The whole
countable vector $(B_{m+k},B_{m+k+1},\ldots)$ is a Borel function of
$\sigma_{2r_{m+k}}\gamma$, because every corresponding outer radius
is at most $2r_{m+k}$. Thus (AS2) gives the upper bound
\[
 C(2r_{m+k}/r_m)^\alpha=C2^{\alpha}2^{-\alpha k}.
\]
Enlarge $C_0$ to cover $1\le k<8$ by the trivial total-variation bound
one. Interchanging the two coordinates of a joint law preserves total
variation, giving (AS4) in the displayed order.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A strong law for bounded annular observations]
\label{cor:scenery-slln}
Let $f_n$ be any deterministic sequence of Borel real functions of an
annular oriented crossing, with $|f_n|\le1$. Put
$Z_n=f_n(B_n)$. Then
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N\{Z_n-\mathbb E_P Z_n\}
          \longrightarrow0\qquad P\text{-almost surely}.
 \tag{AS5}
\]
In particular, for a fixed bounded Borel $f$, if the deterministic
Ces\`aro means
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N\mathbb E_P f(B_n)
\]
converge to $c_f$, then the empirical means
$N^{-1}\sum_{n\le N}f(B_n)$ converge almost surely to $c_f$.
These assertions hold simultaneously for any prescribed countable
collection of choices of the sequence $(f_n)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The total-variation estimate (AS4) and $|Z_n|\le1$ imply
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}_P(Z_n,Z_j)|
       \le2C_0 2^{-\alpha|n-j|}\quad(n\ne j).
\]
Consequently, for $S_N=\sum_{n\le N}(Z_n-\mathbb E_P Z_n)$,
\[
 \mathbb E_P S_N^2\le C_1 N.
\]
Chebyshev's inequality and Borel--Cantelli give
$S_{\ell^2}/\ell^2\to0$ almost surely, since for every $\zeta>0$,
\[
 \sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
 \mathbb P\{|S_{\ell^2}|>\zeta\ell^2\}
 \le\frac{C_1}{\zeta^2}
                  \sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{-2}<\infty.
\]
For $\ell^2\le N<(\ell+1)^2$, boundedness of the centered summands
gives
$|S_N-S_{\ell^2}|\le2(2\ell+1)$. Dividing by $N$ yields (AS5).
Add the deterministic expectations for the second assertion, and
intersect countably many probability-one events for the last one.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Meaning and remaining gap.}
Annular crossing statistics of one limiting exploration self-average
over logarithmically many source scales. The centering sequence is
deterministic, even when the scaling limit itself has not been shown
dilation invariant. Neither the convergence of those deterministic
expectations nor their values is asserted. A source law that is
invariant under dyadic dilation would make the expectations constant,
but such invariance is an additional premise and is not inferred from
precompactness of the collection of scaling limits. The result supplies
a rigorous scale-statistics diagnostic without an affine-SLE ansatz;
it does not classify those deterministic statistics or produce random
slit-domain exploration kernels.
