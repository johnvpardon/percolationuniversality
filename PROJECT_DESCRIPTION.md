Our goal is to prove "unversality" for two-dimensional critical percolation.

A "percolation model" shall mean something like bond or site percolation on a periodic graph embedded in the plane R^2.  For instance, it could be bond or site percolation on the triangular lattice, hexagonal lattice, or square lattice.  More exotic models like Voronoi percolation (site or bond percolation on the Voronoi diagram of a periodic Poisson process on R^2), or site or bond percolation on a Penrose tiling should be fine too, but for simplicity let's leave this off consideration.  Conceptually speaking, we will think of all percolation as *site percolation* (it is not hard to phrase bond percolation as a particular case of site percolation, where some sites are given a constant color).

"Universality" means that the scaling limit at the critical percolation threshold (probability) is independent of the percolation model.  Recall that Smirnov (and later work) has given a complete description of the scaling limit of site percolation on the triangular lattice in terms of Cardy's Formula and SLE_6.  So, we know what limit we are looking for.  Smirnov's proof, however, used a particular property of the triangular lattice which does not hold for all lattices.  I am happy to make "coarse" assumptions about the scaling limit, such as the RSW estimates.  Duminil--Copin and collaborator proved that these hold in many cases whenever the lattice has sufficient symmetry.  We would be happy with a prove of universality conditional on RSW estimates (as this is a coarse property of critical percolation).

Now let us discuss suggested lines of approach.  Consider the "exploration path" random process.  That is, given a simply connected bounded domain Omega inside R^2 and two prime ends of it ("prime end" just means "boundary point" when Omega has smooth boundary; roughly speaking "prime end" means boundary point of the hyperbolic disk biholomorphic to Omega), we can consider the "exploration path" which is the random path between the two prime ends given by the scaling limit of the percolation model, where we impose "white" boundary conditions on one arc and "black" boundary conditions on the other.  Due to RSW estimates (which we are assuming), this scaling limit is reasonable, and we call it \gamma_{\Omega,p,q}.  Note that this exploration path \gamma_{\Omega,p,q} is an *unparameterized* path (note the scaling limit exploration path is a limit of embedded paths, but itself need not be embedded, rather it satisfies a weaker and somewhat complicated "may touch but may not cross" property).  Now the random exploration paths \gamma_{\Omega,p,q} satisfy the following two Markov properties:
- (Exploration Path Markov Property) If we fix a partial exploration path starting at p but not (yet) reaching q, rather stopping at p', then the distribution of the remaining path from p' to q is the same as the distribution of the exploration path for (\Omega\setminus(fixed path from p to p'),p',q).
- (Domain Markov Property) If (\Omega',p,q) is obtained from (\Omega,p,q) by removing some closed set K disjoint from p and q, then the exploration path processes of the two are *the same* up until the point that they hit K.
- (Translation Invariance) Translating (Omega,p,q) translates \gamma_{\Omega,p,q} in the same way.
It seems quite plausible that one can simply classify all ensembles of random paths {\gamma_{\Omega,p,q}} (that is, collections of random paths \gamma_{Omega,p,q} indexed by (Omega,p,q)) satisfying these two Markov properties.  In fact, it seems quite likely that there is a *single* such ensemble.  This is quite intuitive: along most of the path gamma_{Omega,p,q}, it lies away from the boundary of Omega, and hence by the two Markov properties its behavior is purely "local" and universal (independent of Omega,p,q).  As a (very elementary) analogy, recall that there is a (rather elementary) classification of Markov processes on R valued in R: they are all given by a deterministic drift plus Brownian motion with some (possibly varying) intensity.  We hope to obtain a similar classification of ensembles of random paths {\gamma_{\Omega,p,q}} indexed by (Omega,p,q).

To classify ensembles of random paths \gamma_{Omega,p,q} indexed by (Omega,p,q) satisfying the two Markov properties above (and translation invariance), it is almost certainly helpful to parameterize each path gamma_{Omega,p,q} by its *Loewner parameterization* (as is traditional in this field), which is a (random) function \Gamma_{\Omega,p,q}:\RR_{\geq 0}\to\RR (the "Loewner driving function").  Note that this function \Gamma_{Omega,p,q} is *not* (at least not a priori) a Markov process (although a posteriori we may prove that it is Brownian motion, corresponding to \gamma_{Omega,p,q} being chordal SLE_6): the two Markov properties and translation invariance correspond to somewhat complicated conditions on the random functions \Gamma_{Omega,p,q}.  Also note that we absolutely *do not* assume (a priori) any sort of conformal invariance of \gamma_{Omega,p,q}!!  Rather, we would like to *conclude* conformal invariance by showing that the only ensemble of random paths satisfying these properties is SLE_6.

Really, the classification of (ensembeles indexed by (Omega,p,q) of) random functions \Gamma_{\Omega,p,q}:\RR_{\geq 0}\to\RR, satisfying the two Markov properties and translation invariance, is the *core question* underlying the ultimate universality goal.  We should work towards this classification first, and achieving it would be sufficient to declare success.  Probably proving universality from such a classification would be long and technical, but ultimately not the most conceptually interesting (of course, it's interesting nontrivial work which deserves to be completed, but it is secondary in importance to providing a complete proof of the classification of random paths satisfying the conditions above).
