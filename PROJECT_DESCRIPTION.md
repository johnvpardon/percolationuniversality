Our goal is to prove "unversality" for two-dimensional critical percolation.

A "percolation model" shall mean something like bond or site percolation on a periodic graph embedded in the plane R^2.  For instance, it could be bond or site percolation on the triangular lattice, hexagonal lattice, or square lattice.  More exotic models like Voronoi percolation (site or bond percolation on the Voronoi diagram of a periodic Poisson process on R^2), or site or bond percolation on a Penrose tiling should be fine too, but for simplicity let's leave this off consideration.  Conceptually speaking, we will think of all percolation as *site percolation* (it is not hard to phrase bond percolation as a particular case of site percolation, where some sites are given a constant color).

"Universality" means that the scaling limit at the critical percolation threshold (probability) is independent of the percolation model.  Recall that Smirnov (and later work) has given a complete description of the scaling limit of site percolation on the triangular lattice in terms of Cardy's Formula and SLE_6.  So, we know what limit we are looking for.  Smirnov's proof, however, used a particular property of the triangular lattice which does not hold for all lattices.  I am happy to make "coarse" assumptions about the scaling limit, such as the RSW estimates.  Duminil--Copin and collaborator proved that these hold in many cases whenever the lattice has sufficient symmetry.  We would be happy with a prove of universality conditional on RSW estimates (as this is a coarse property of critical percolation).

Now let us discuss suggested lines of approach.  Consider the "exploration path" random process.  That is, given a simply connected bounded domain Omega inside R^2 and two prime ends of it ("prime end" just means "boundary point" when Omega has smooth boundary; roughly speaking "prime end" means boundary point of the hyperbolic disk biholomorphic to Omega), we can consider the "exploration path" which is the random path between the two prime ends given by the scaling limit of the percolation model, where we impose "white" boundary conditions on one arc and "black" boundary conditions on the other.  Due to RSW estimates (which we are assuming), this scaling limit is reasonable.  Note that this exploration path is an *unparameterized* path, however we can (and do usually) give it its canonical Loewner parameterization, as is customary in this field (note that in the limit, the paths we get may not be embedded, rather they satisfy a somewhat complicated "may touch but may not cross" property).  We therefore have, for every domain Omega and every ordered pair of prime ends p and q, a random function \gamma_{\Omega,p,q}:\RR_{\geq 0}\to\RR, namely the Loewner driving function associated to the exploration path on \Omega from p to q.  Now \gamma_{Omega,p,q} is not quite a Markov process, however it does satisfy the following very similar properties:
- (Exploration Path Markov Property) If we fix a partial exploration path starting at p but not (yet) reaching q, rather stopping at p', then the distribution of the remaining path from p' to q is the same as the distribution of the exploration path for (\Omega\setminus(fixed path from p to p'),p',q).
- (Domain Markov Property) If (\Omega',p,q) is obtained from (\Omega,p,q) by removing some closed set K disjoint from p and q, then the exploration path processes of the two are *the same* up until the point that they hit K.
These two Markov properties can be translated into properties of the collection of random processes \gamma_{\Omega,p,q}.  They are, of course, not quite Markov processes, but nevertheless the above two constraints are quite strong.

There is a (rather elementary) classification of Markov processes on R valued in R: they are all given by a deterministic drift plus Brownian motion with some (possibly varying) intensity.  It is reasonable to ask for a classification of all random processes {\gamma_{\Omega,p,q}} (that is, collections of random functions \gamma_{Omega,p,q} for all (Omega,p,q)) satisfying the above two Markov properties.  This is really the *core* of the question!  In fact, such a classification would really count as equivalent to achieving the goal of universality.  Probably proving universality from such a classification would be long and technical, but ultimately not the most conceptually interesting.  The really interesting question that we should focus on is the classification of {\gamma_{\Omega,p,q}} satisfying the above pair of Markov properties.  Really SLE_6 should be the unique answer here (note that, in this case, each \gamma_{\Omega,p,q} actually does turn out to be a Markov process on R, but this is a priori quite mysterious from the perspective of the above two Markov properties, which concern the domain and the path, rather than its Loewner parameterization! note that we are *absolutely not* assuming any sort of a priori conformal invariance of {\gamma_{\Omega,p,q}}!).
