Our goal is to prove "unversality" for two-dimensional critical percolation.

A "percolation model" shall mean something like bond or site percolation on a periodic graph embedded in the plane R^2.  For instance, it could be bond or site percolation on the triangular lattice, hexagonal lattice, or square lattice.  More exotic models like Voronoi percolation (site or bond percolation on the Voronoi diagram of a periodic Poisson process on R^2), or site or bond percolation on a Penrose tiling should be fine too, but for simplicity let's leave this off consideration.  Conceptually speaking, we will think of all percolation as *site percolation* (it is not hard to phrase bond percolation as a particular case of site percolation, where some sites are given a constant color).

"Universality" means that the scaling limit at the critical percolation threshold (probability) is independent of the percolation model.  Recall that Smirnov (and later work) has given a complete description of the scaling limit of site percolation on the triangular lattice in terms of Cardy's Formula and SLE_6.  So, we know what limit we are looking for.  Smirnov's proof, however, used a particular property of the triangular lattice which does not hold for all lattices.  I am happy to make "coarse" assumptions about the scaling limit, such as the RSW estimates.  Duminil--Copin and collaborator proved that these hold in many cases whenever the lattice has sufficient symmetry.  We would be happy with a prove of universality conditional on RSW estimates (or on any other "coarse property" of critical percolation, though other than RSW there's not much else of this nature); note that RSW guarantees the existence of subsequential scaling limits.  Note that there is an obvious symmetry (x,y)-->(x,ay) (fixed real a>0) of R^2 which acts on lattices and on scaling limits.  Universality of the scaling limit must be understood as modulo some unknown affine transformation of R^2 depending on the lattice (this reparameterization is forced to be trivial for lattices with enough symmetry, like triangular or square lattices, which is why we don't see it in the simplest examples of percolation, but for asymmetric lattices it will certainly be necessary).

Now let us discuss suggested lines of approach.  Consider the "exploration path" random process.  That is, given a simply connected bounded domain Omega inside R^2 and three prime ends (x,p,q) [in this cyclic order] of it ("prime end" just means "boundary point" when Omega has smooth boundary; roughly speaking "prime end" means boundary point of the hyperbolic disk biholomorphic to Omega), we can consider the "exploration path" which is the random path from x to the p-q segment given by the scaling limit of the percolation model, where we impose "white" boundary conditions on the x-p segment and "black" boundary conditions on the x-q segment (and no boundary conditions on the p-q segment.  Due to RSW estimates (which we are assuming), this scaling limit is reasonable, and we call it \gamma_{\Omega,x,p,q}.  Note that this exploration path \gamma_{\Omega,x,p,q} is an *unparameterized* path (note the scaling limit exploration path is a limit of embedded paths, but itself need not be embedded, rather it satisfies a weaker and somewhat complicated "may touch but may not cross" property).  Now the random exploration paths \gamma_{\Omega,x,p,q} satisfy the following four properties:
- (Domain Markov Property) If we fix a partial exploration path starting at x but not (yet) reaching p-q segment, rather stopping at x', then the distribution of the remaining path from x' to p-q is the same as the distribution of the exploration path for (\Omega\setminus(fixed path from x to x'),x',p,q).
- (Locality) If (\Omega',x,p',q') and (\Omega,x,p,q) coincide in a neighborhood of x, then the exploration path processes of the two are *the same* up until the point that they exit this neighborhood.
- (Translation Invariance) Translating (Omega,x,p,q) translates \gamma_{\Omega,x,p,q} in the same way.
- RSW estimates: probability of hitting any given open subinterval of the p-q arc is positive.
It seems quite plausible that one can simply classify all ensembles of random paths {\gamma_{\Omega,x,p,q}} (that is, collections of random paths \gamma_{Omega,x,p,q} indexed by (Omega,x,p,q)) satisfying these four properties.  In fact, it seems quite likely that there is a *single* such ensemble (up to affine transformations of R^2, as discussed above).  This is quite intuitive: along most of the path gamma_{Omega,x,p,q}, it lies away from the boundary of Omega, and hence by the four properties its behavior is purely "local" and universal (independent of Omega,x,p,q).  As a (very elementary) analogy, recall that there is a (rather elementary) classification of Markov processes on R valued in R: they are all given by a deterministic drift (possibly depending on time and position) plus Brownian motion with some (again possibly varying) intensity.  We hope to obtain a similar classification of ensembles of random paths {\gamma_{\Omega,x,p,q}} indexed by (Omega,x,p,q).

To classify ensembles of random paths \gamma_{Omega,x,p,q} indexed by (Omega,x,p,q) satisfying the four properties above, it is almost certainly helpful to parameterize each path gamma_{Omega,x,p,q} by its *Loewner parameterization* (as is traditional in this field), which is a (random) function \Gamma_{\Omega,x,p,q}:\RR_{\geq 0}\to\RR (the "Loewner driving function").  Note that this function \Gamma_{Omega,x,p,q} is *not* (at least not a priori) a Markov process (although a posteriori we may prove that it is Brownian motion, corresponding to \gamma_{Omega,x,p,q} being SLE_6): the four properties above correspond to somewhat complicated conditions on the random functions \Gamma_{Omega,x,p,q}.  Also note that we absolutely *do not* assume (a priori) any sort of conformal invariance of \gamma_{Omega,x,p,q}!!  Rather, we would like to *conclude* conformal invariance by showing that the only ensemble of random paths satisfying these properties is SLE_6.  Note that the identification with SLE_6 and the a posteriori Markov property will depend on choosing the correct affine transformation identifying R^2 with C, and that choosing the "wrong" one will certainly result in \Gamma_{Omega,x,p,q} not being SLE_6 (and probably not even being Markov).

Really, the classification of (ensembeles indexed by (Omega,x,p,q) of) random functions \Gamma_{\Omega,x,p,q}:\RR_{\geq 0}\to\RR, satisfying the four properties above, is the *core question* underlying the ultimate universality goal.  We should work towards this classification first, and achieving it would be sufficient to declare success.  Probably proving universality from such a classification would be long and technical, but ultimately not the most conceptually interesting (of course, it's interesting nontrivial work which deserves to be completed, but it is secondary in importance to providing a complete proof of the classification of random paths satisfying the conditions above).  To be completely precise, we wish to prove (or disprove):
CONJECTURE: Any ensembele of random functions \Gamma_{\Omega,x,p,q}:\RR_{\geq 0}\to\RR indexed by (Omega,x,p,q) satisfying the four properties above (domain markov, locality, translation invariance) is SLE_6 (up to a fixed affine transformation of R^2).

Note that there are certainly well definedness issues surrounding the Loewner correspondence for "rough" functions, such as SLE_6.  We expect these to be technical annoyances, rather than central issues.  In particular, we allow to formulate the above conjecture precisely using any reasonable translation of the four properties of \gamma_{Omega,x,p,q} into corresponding properties of \Gamma_{Omega,x,p,q} (for example, translate these conditions on random *smooth embedded* paths gamma into corresponding conditions on Gamma, which works since for smooth functions the Loewner correspondence is a reasonable bijection).
